Hey Andrea Unfortunately you’re right, a plantar fasciotomy cannot be physically reversed. Once the plantar fascia is surgically cut or partially released, the tissue fibers separate, and the original structure cannot be sewn back together or restored to its original state. Instead of a structural reversal, managing the complications or "reversing" the negative side effects of a plantar fasciotomy relies on structural support, physical rehabilitation and or revisional procedures. They say treatment focuses on stabilizing the foot eg - custom orthotics to support your arch that’s collapsing. Cutting the fascia can cause the medial longitudinal arch to drop or flatten. Custom orthotics provide the external mechanical support that the severed ligament can no longer supply. You may need them for a very long time. Physical Therapy: you may need specialized exercise that focus on strengthening the intrinsic muscles of the foot and the posterior tibial tendon to pick up the slack for the weakened arch. Releasing too much of the fascia often shifts excess weight to the outside of the foot (the lateral column). Podiatric physical therapy or specialized footwear modifications are used to re-distribute this pressure. This Reversal surgery. I think they might be referring to - Revisional Surgery: In cases where severe pain persists due to aggressive scar tissue formation or an incomplete initial release, a specialist may perform a revision surgery to clean up scar tissue rather than recreate the ligament. That’s the definition above ⬆️. That’s how to treat what has happened. But I would continue down the path you are doing with the other podiatrist etc. I’m sorry I can’t support you further on the legal side of things.
