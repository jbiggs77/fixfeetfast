My son had bunion surgery and also had the hammertoe next to the bunion corrected on December 23rd.

Archive date 2026-10-1514 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryHammer Toe Surgery & CorrectionPost-Surgery Swelling & InflammationWalking & Weight Bearing After Surgery

My son had bunion surgery and also had the hammertoe next to the bunion corrected on December 23rd. The doctor told him to start gently moving his foot up and down and wiggling his toes. His stitches have been removed, and we were told he can wash the incision area, but not scrub it with a washcloth. I wanted to ask: when does the swelling usually go down after this type of surgery? When did you start moving your foot up and down and wiggling your toes? What helps? Also, when is it typically safe to start walking on it again and driving? For anyone who has had bunion and hammertoe surgery, how long did it take before you were able to walk normally, drive again, and see the swelling go down? And is there anything that helps the swelling go down faster? He is in a boot now.

Community comment#1

Swelling can last for up to a year. His foot looks great! Have him elevate as much as possible, wear compression socks, and do lymphedema massage (super easy). His surgeon will tell him/you when it's safe to walk on it and drive. Everyone's procedures are different.

Community comment#2

I had bunion and hammertoe surgery on November 28. I am in a boot at 7 weeks. Next week, I can try a regular shoe. I am wiggling my toes but they are stiff and foot is swollen. Since my surgery was on my left foot, I have been driving after 3 weeks. When I had surgery on my right foot 10 years ago, I could not drive until I could wear a regular shoe.

Community comment#3

I started the toe wiggle 7 days in. Elevation and ice and I have no swelling.

Community comment#4

I had surgery on 12/9, just this week started in a walking boot. I have a lot of swelling in upper foot and toes as well. Doctor said to continue to elevate, compression sock (covers toes and goes up to knees), and ice. No driving. Doctor said about 3 weeks ago to continue to move the foot up and down, side to side m, rotate on a circle and wiggle the toes. The upper part of my foot to my toes are very swollen and stiff/numb. Im also using some red light therapy on my foot. Best of luck to your son, this post op from surgery has not been fun.

Community comment#5

￼ Swelling is highly variable dependent on patient physiology and activity level. From a trauma surgery perspective I used to tell patients to ice 20 minutes on 20 minutes off with the extremity elevated above heart level as many times as they can throughout the day as this will significantly reduce swelling and by proxy pain.￼ Most practices, have a standard from a legal liability standpoint of about six weeks postop before you can reliably use your right foot to mash on the brakes.￼

Community comment#6

My surgery was 12/10 and I’m still in a boot. It still swells if I’m on it for a short period of time. Elevate, ice,ibuprofen helps with swelling as well.

Community comment#7

I had surgery 4/9/25. I was sent home in a simple wound dressing and a wedged shoe (heel walking). I started pt in-home by myself on day 1 and attended in-person at 2wks post-op, which really helped with healing. Elevating when seated/laying down, applying cold therapy (not ice) behind the knee and directly on the forefoot helps relieve/decrease both pain and swelling. Be sure to have him elevate both legs/feet in order to keep his hips in alignment. Wearing a compression stocking during the day, every single day, helps with the swelling as well. Massaging and flexing helps, too! Is he taking any prescribed or otc pain meds? None of us can advise you on when he should start weight bearing. I’d say follow the surgeon or physical therapist’s recommendation on that. Check out YouTube. It has a lot of videos on different pt exercises after bunion surgery.

Community comment#8

Big toe fusion on dec 5. Also Morton's Neuroma removal. My Dr was super pleased with xrays yesterday. 2 more weeks with boot. I can use slippers part of the time. Still swollen and red. I still elevate and ice, because it reduces the achiness. Dr said, swelling will gradually reduce over next months. She said driving is fine since it's my left foot, after I stopped the pain meds (was 4 days for me). Check with your insurance tho... I do a little bit of toe exercises, wiggles and low key stretches. I believe they are normally recommended. If he gets a fever, new pain, full body aches, or heat in foot call Dr. ASAP.

Community comment#9

The swelling can last up to a year. I started moving my foot up and done once I was given clearance from my surgeon. The swelling will ramp up once he starts walking again so I iced and elevated after each of my walks each time to keep swelling down. I don’t remember the exact date but maybe a few weeks out or so. And walking and driving will depend on what type of surgery he had and when his surgeon states he can. It varies because of how many different type of bunion surgeries there are. Driving, wasn’t told until I am comfortable making a complete and sudden stop.

Community comment#10

I think the question really is how our bodies handle soft tissue damage or disturbances, nerve damage and disturbances. It takes a long time to recuperate but the initial healing is pretty quickly. Lots of burning and nerve sensations your foot falls asleep quicker seems like the blood flow is off. But it usually goes down quite a lot by 6 to 8 weeks. And it does go by fast
