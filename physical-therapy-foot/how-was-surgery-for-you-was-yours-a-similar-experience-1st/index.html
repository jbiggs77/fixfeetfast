Archive date 2026-10-0350 archived replies
Minimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Hammer Toe Surgery & CorrectionPain Management After Foot Surgery

How was surgery for you? Was yours a similar experience: 1st step: 10+ shots in the foot for “nerve blocker.” 2. No anesthesia. Just wore sound canceling headphones phones. 3. Laid on a flat surgical table; chose not to look, but I could have chosen to watch the whole thing. 4. Doctor had a hand-held x-ray machine. (No scope inside the foot.) She did many x-rays throughout the procedure. She would lift my foot (with my help) onto a small platform next to the surgical table; take an x-ray, and then put my foot back on the table to work further. This was disturbing because when she did this I could see my blood dripping from her gloved hands. (Maybe this wouldn’t bother some people, but it bothered me!) 5.My feeling kept coming back and she had to give me more and more shots. By the end, I just put up with dull pain until she was done. My feeling was FULLY back within a half hour (or less) after surgery. I am experiencing great trepidation about going back for my other foot. I wish I could just be put under anesthesia or SOMETHING! Just wondering if this is pretty normal and I’m just a big wimp, or what?!

Community comment#23

I love this post! Raises a bunch of questions and uncertainties that most doctors don’t address with their patients. First, sounds like your experience was not good, something no one should have to deal with - especially without knowing. I will preface by saying that I perform this surgery daily without general anesthesia, the numbing part, injections, depending on how many toes are being worked on is the worst. No doubt. Takes less than 20 seconds normally, and 5 pokes. I will weigh pros and cons from my experience, but admittedly, this is not that of the patient. What I, personally offer, is different than what you’ve had, and more similar to sedation dentistry - offering Valium, lorazepam for anxiety, and nitrous oxide/laughing gas, to help with the pain of the injections. Nonetheless, here is what I have found. General anesthesia- don’t feel the foot injections. That’s a positive. In my experience, that’s about it. The negatives I see - cost, anesthesia comes with it the cost of the doctor, in addition, the cost of the often required tests - lab draw, (which is also more needle sticks), urine test, chest x-rays, EKG, not to mention the IV poke - which I have needed for other surgeries (took 3 attempts to get a good vein that didn’t roll, or blow - bruised arm for 3 wks). If other comorbidities - diabetes, fasting leads to medicine adjustments, if blood thinners, often heparin bridge which is needle pokes to stomach 1-3 times daily for 1-4 wks. If RA - often need a C-spine x-ray, and medicine adjustments if on anti rejection medications. Also, the fasting. And recovery. Your experience of having numbing not last long, is not what my patients get at all. My patients are often numb for 24-48hrs. Also, they never wake up out of anesthesia in pain, because 1, I they are never put to sleep, and 2, I could not perform the surgery with them awake without a good block. I have heard horror stories of patient la waking up during the surgery in pain, as well as in the recovery area in pain with a cast on that needed a further block and a cast change. Some docs insert popliteal pain catheters in the pre-op area, which is often ultrasound guided, but another poke or too. I’m sorry you had the experience, definitely not good. But, I will say I have patients travel from all over, 17 states last year, and other countries because I don’t use general anesthesia (I also don’t use hardware) and let my patients walk - typically takes under an hour, and some fly home same day). General anesthesia comes with added risks of pneumonia, chipped teeth from intubation, scratched cornea from taping your eyes closed, stroke, UTI (if a catheter is used), amongst needing a ride home and hours/days in hospital. Some younger patients susceptible to malignant hyperthermia, can be fatal. All these rare, but zero if not put to sleep. If you want your other foot fixed, but don’t want to be put under, feel free to reach out. We offer free phone/email consults.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#36

yes, a good local block should last half a day minimum. Possibly your doc used a short acting anesthetic, or not enough, or didn’t target all of the nerve branches. I think for foot surgery, arthroscopy is rare, as need to inject fluid in the joints to give space for a camera - more portals for fluid, camera, and instrument to work with. Good to clean up joints, not fixing bunions or hammertoes. Laparoscopic involves insufflating air, good for abdominal surgery, also not bunions. Fluoroscopy, live x-ray, is great for bunions because you can use metallic instruments and see what you’re doing with the bone, without added incisions. So, I like that, and use daily. Hope this helps!
