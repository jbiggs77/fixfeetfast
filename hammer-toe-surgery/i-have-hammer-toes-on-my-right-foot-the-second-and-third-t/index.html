I have hammer toes on my right foot - the second and third toes. I am unable use any corrective devices because those 2 toes are webbed! So, it seems that surgery is my only alternative. I would like to know what kinds of surgeries can be done, which are less invasive, and which have the easiest, or shortest recovery time.

Community comment#1

I too had2nd/3rd toe webbing so taping wasn’t very helpful in my case either. The tips of my toes were clawing when I walked and eroding on the tips, scary and painful. Try soft tissue surgeries first like tendon lengthening or tendon transfer. Don’t let your doctor talk you into permanent screws. (I have one in the tip of my second toe. It has all but crippled me.) I had to have a second surgery to pull the middle joint down and shorten it significantly. It will always affect my gait but at least I can fit it in a shoe. Unfortunately now, my big toe is turning inward and sitting on top of it. Such a mess. I advise soft tissue correction if possible. (That’s been my experience.)

Community comment#4

Do you know why you are having hammer toes. People develop them for a variety of different reasons. They are a result of compensations in other parts of your body.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#5

I have high arches. The left started to fall about 10 years ago. Also my second and third toe are webbed half way. Since I started playing sports on hard surfaces (junior high), I had prominent metatarsal heads under 2nd and 3rd toes. About 8 years ago; I began supinating really bad on my left foot and injured something under my 4th toe (plantar plate I believe). Then I couldn’t roll up on that toe. They did osteotomies on my three middle toes. Only problem was that they lifted toes 2 and 3 but only shortened toe 4. Now toe 4 is prominent and I have been told I need a lifting osteotomy to help take weight off that. Hence I shuffle on. Also now my big toe is getting very painful with hallux rigidus and start of a bunion. It’s hard to walk without pain currently.
