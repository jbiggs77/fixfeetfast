I'm so confused and stressed about whether or not to have surgery.

Archive date 2026-09-1441 archived replies
Lapiplasty 3D Bunion SurgeryHammer Toe Surgery & CorrectionBest Shoes After Foot SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot ExercisesFlat Feet & Arch Support

I'm so confused and stressed about whether or not to have surgery. I'm in the UK and don't have the money to go private. I had a consultation in January 2025 and the doc said I should have surgery in next 5 years in case I develop arthritis and the operation could result in lack of mobility in big toe. That freaked me out but I went away to think about it as I wasn't ready to think about surgery. My bunions don't look too bad and they're fine this time of year mostly as I'm in sandals but they ache in winter and effect my mobility with exercising, so I can't do lunges at the gym and my squatting stance is off and they effect yoga too. They were worst they've ever been last winter and hurt if I wear anything other than super stretchy trainers for longer than an hour. I love hiking and can't wear my hiking boots for long periods (they have a reasonably wide toe box that was fine for me up until last year). I've had bunions since my 20s (I'm 50 now) and it's only the past year that they've felt bad enough to consider an op. I decided to go back and arrange to have them operated on but have just been to a different doctor (after having to wait 8 months for an appointment) and he didn't seem as insistent that I had them operated on and just kept highlighting all that could go wrong. I know he has to let me know the risks but it's left me more confused than before as I had decided it was the best option and now I don't know what to do! I also thought I may be able to have some choice as to when I have the surgery and was hoping to get it out of the way over December as I'm self employed and that's my quietest time of the year but the waitlist is 9 months. I'm so worried about not being able to work for months being single and self employed and not rich!! Sorry for the essay. I'm wondering how others decided to have the op? What factors finally made you decide to have it? Those of you who are glad you had it done, please tell me why. Please please don't share any horror stories on this post though as I have health anxiety.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#41

the most common cause of recurrence is failing to address the unstable joint at the base of the metatarsal bone. Many patients also struggle with ligamentous laxity, or hypermobility. This means the connective tissues are naturally more flexible, making it easier for the bones to drift out of alignment again if they aren't properly secured during the procedure. The "recurance" is usually only if the bone is just shaved and the whole issue cause the bunion is not fixed properly. Triplanar 3D corrections have reported recurrence rates as low as 0.8% in recent 2025 studies.
