Community comment#3

My bunions were worse than yours and were causing hammertoes to form. They didn’t hurt all the time so I could have waited but I knew they would get worse and I decided it would be easier to do them now than down the road when I was older and it was harder to get around anyway. I had the left foot done March 20th of this year (chevron akin bunion and hammertoe surgery). My foot healed great so I had the right foot done with the same surgery on July 14th. I saw an orthopedic surgeon who was great and does many of them. Now I will say the first few weeks are awful (still less than two weeks out from my right foot surgery). I was in a surgical shoe from day one and could weight bear as tolerated. I use the knee scooter a lot right now. But for reference my left foot was done March 20th and May 26th-May 30th I was at Walt Disney World and did tons of walking. On my left foot I still mainly wear Hoca shoes for comfort because there is a slight bit of swelling so imagine that will be the case with the right. Something that helped the healing with the left foot was going to OrthoLazer. I am not sure if they have one in your town (I am in Texas), but I truly believe it helped. Good luck!!

Community comment#4

I don’t regret having bunion surgery, but I do think it’s important to go in with realistic expectations. For me, the deciding factor would have been when the pain started affecting everyday activities and conservative treatments were no longer helping. One of the best things you can do is find a surgeon who performs bunion surgeries regularly, ask what specific procedure they recommend for your foot, and discuss the expected recovery timeline and long-term outcomes. Recovery is often longer than people expect, with swelling that can last for months, but many people are happy they had it because their pain improves and they can return to activities more comfortably. I wish I’d known how important patience is during recovery and how much following post-op instructions and physical therapy can affect the outcome. I’d definitely ask your podiatrist why they’re recommending a particular procedure over others, what your expected foot function will be afterward, and what the risks are based on your specific bunion and lifestyle. Those answers can make the decision much clearer. Also you can check out Essentials of Joint Health by Joe Zapata on Amazon for that extra tips on joints and surgeries
