I am in a quandary.

Archive date 2026-09-2613 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryHammer Toe Surgery & CorrectionBest Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After Surgery

I am in a quandary. I went to see the surgeon and yes qualify for surgery due to the bunion. But I was steered away from surgery as the surgeon feels that unless you are in severe pain , it not worth risking the10% failure rate and long recovery . I watched my grandmother become disabled due to her bunion issues and don’t want that to happen to me . Am 57 , already had a hip replacement but back playing pickleball and walking . Bunions throb, score against the extra wide trainers / runners . But the severity is increasing greatly Any thoughts?

Community comment#2

I’m sitting here day 5 post op, bilateral bunionectomy, 2nd toe osteomy and a plantar plate repair. I’m solo and I live alone and never did I think I could endure the pain and inconvenience of it. My feet throbbed and ached and some days in winter the thought of wearing shoes made me want to cry. My nanna had awful painful feet and so has my mum. My mum can barely walk at 67. I am super fit and was so active until my feet slowed me down after last year navigating through a stress fracture that hasn’t healed properly. It’s a seriously personal decision that only you can make. But what I will say is you are stronger and more capable than you realised, the inconvenience of surgery and recovery is a small part of it. I’m 49

Community comment#3

I had my right foot done (uk) Nov 24, my mum has shocking feet at the age of 91 and apparently both my grandmas had them. I’m glad I had it done although my foot looks 10x better than it did, I do still have a bump. But I did bump it 3 months post op. Recent X-rays showed it’s all good the bump is soft tissue. The recovery was definitely longer & trickier than I anticipated and definitely harder than everything I read on the internet. Not too painful but frustrating not being able to do things. Then I’ve had Achilles problems in my opposite leg needing treatment. And now told I have a Morton’s neuroma in my opposite leg needing foot do awaiting a scan. It’s not easy. I’m 53 but didn’t want feet like my mum in my older age

Community comment#5

Lisa, I left it hoping that I could avoid bunion surgery like my mother did with similar feet. My bunions gradually widened & increased in size and I got used to the look of them. I bought lots of shoes trying to find comfortable ones without much luck! Unexpectedly at 73, I developed extremely painful bursitis on my left bunion. Fortunately my podiatrist as well as my GP both referred me to an experienced orthopaedic foot & ankle surgeon. I have had a good result, no regrets … over the last 18 months, 3 operations because I suited me to have one foot done at a time and then a hammer toe & tailors bunion correction. I would have been better off when 15 years younger if I had bunion surgery because my general health was much better then. I hope you find a specialist foot & ankle surgeon with whom you feel confident. Good luck. 🤞
