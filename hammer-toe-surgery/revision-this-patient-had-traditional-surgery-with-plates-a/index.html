Revision. This patient had traditional surgery with plates and screws. You can see the scars. She was left with a bunion, painful calluses, and toes curling beneath each other. Now she is 8wks post-op MIS revision surgery. I was able to work around the hardware and no screws or plates were used to revise this. She walked out of the operating room, no general anesthesia, small bandage for under a month. Now, she can walk pain free.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#11

the bones are cut, just not fixated with screws, plates or wires. By angulating the cuts with a small burr, balancing the surrounding soft tissues, and applying an external bandage or splint (think K-tape) your body naturally heals itself in a better, more balanced position. Instead of rigidly holding a position against your body’s natural forces, the deforming forces are rebalanced. Sure, if there’s a trauma, or injury, plates and screws are great to restore the position that was injured, but that’s assuming the position was good before the injury. But if you have a deformity (bunion, hammertoe) that grows over time, I prefer to rebalance and preserve motion without fixation.
