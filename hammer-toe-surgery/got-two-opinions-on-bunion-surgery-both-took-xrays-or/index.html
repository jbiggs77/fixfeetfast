Got two opinions on bunion surgery.

Archive date 2026-09-2514 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryLapiplasty 3D Bunion SurgeryHammer Toe Surgery & Correction

Got two opinions on bunion surgery. Both took x-rays. Orthopedic surgeon specializing in foot and ankle - recommended 1st MTP arthrodesis (fusion) - opinion being that my big toe only has 30% movement currently (although it seems like I am able to move it). With that procedure, they address the arthritis and will also fix bunion. My podiatrist recommended lapiplasty (he doesn’t seem to think there’s too much arthritis) and believes I do have movement currently and doesn’t think a fusion should be the “first course action.” But would have me sign a consent prior to lapiplasty in the event that during that lapiplasty surgery it makes sense to do the 1st MTP arthrodesis. I also have a hammer toe which both would correct.

Community comment#1

Who I chose Lapipasty: Superior Fixation: The procedure uses two small, anatomically-shaped titanium plates that secure the foundational joint in multiple planes, much more securely than the single plate or screws used in traditional Lapidus procedures. This provides immediate structural stability. Addressing the Root Cause: The Lapiplasty procedure corrects the entire metatarsal bone in three dimensions, returning it to its normal anatomical alignment, rather than just cutting and shifting the bone in two dimensions. This proper alignment, combined with stable fixation, creates a more stable overall structure. Stimulating Healing: Early, protected weight-bearing actually helps stimulate the bone healing process in the correct position, which is beneficial for recovery. Reduced Motion at Surgical Site: The secure, biplanar plating minimizes motion at the surgical site, which, in traditional methods, would compromise healing and require a longer non-weight bearing period in a cast. While patients can begin walking in a surgical boot within days to a couple of weeks, the bone and soft tissues still need several months to fully heal before returning to high-impact activities. The ability to bear weight early is a significant advantage in the recovery process, allowing for a quicker return to daily life activities compared to traditional surgery, which often requires 6-8 weeks of strict non-weight-bearing. I hope this helps

Community comment#6

What’s concerning is if you do a lapiplasty, if you later need big toe fusion you’d then have that entire section of your foot fused which isn’t recommended. I have a very small bunion on my right big toe but the arthritis in my big toe is a bigger issue. All four surgeons I consulted with said they wouldn’t recommend lapiplasty for me if my bunion got worse bc I may eventually need big toe fusion. But maybe there’s another reason the podiatrist thinks lapiplasty is appropriate? Like you are hyper mobile? Personally I would get a few more opinions. I wouldn’t be excited to get lapiplasty if I also have big toe arthritis. You can also ask about doing a cheilectomy with an osteotemy to address both the limited range of motion and the bunion without fusing any joints. If that doesn’t work then you could revisit a fusion approach
