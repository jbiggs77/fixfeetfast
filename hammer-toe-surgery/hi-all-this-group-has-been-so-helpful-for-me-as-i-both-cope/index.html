I’d love to hear from more who had both hammer toe surg with pin and and the mis bunion done together… should I expect to be on crutches a lot more due to coupling with toe? Community comment#1

I had both feet done at the same time, bunion correction and 2nd toe hammertoe correction, screws in all affected toes (which will remain in situ). Surgery was 28th July and I ‘walked’ out of hospital. Never used crutches as I’m useless on them, especially with two ‘bad’ feet. Was never given a boot. I did rest for pretty much two weeks, which I found agonising as I’m so active. I have a desk job so just worked from home. I was told to wear whatever is comfy (nothing 😂😂) so been wearing adjustable Birkenstocks but can now get wide toe trainers on 🎉 Feet still swell and there is a degree of pain sometimes but nothing too bad. Did my first yoga class last week and now starting up Pilates again, not much I haven’t been able to do. Hope this helps.

Community comment#7

I had MIS surgery on my bunion with 2 screws, and hammertoe surgery. I was in bed for two weeks (99% of the time) elevating and icing 20 minutes of every hour. Up only to bathroom for first 48 hours, then only to move to couch and sit with it elevated. I was very strict with this and had little swelling and little pain. After the prescribed meds finished at one week, needed no more meds, not even Tylenol. Week 3 I started walking again, first 5 minutes and then worked up to 15 minutes 3 times a day. I used a cardboard box with foot hole cut out to keep the covers off my foot. I took ice to the surgery and iced and elevated on the way home. Having your foot below your heart causes swelling so being on your feet will cause swelling and pain. So I did not use crutches as I was not up out of bed much. these were the directions from my doctor and it worked, but follow what your doctor tells you. 6 months in all is good and I am very happy to have done it.

Yes the screws were left in. I had the box so when I turned over in the night, the covers did not get caught. I was given a surgical boot to wear, and could have taken it off, but was told by the doctor absolutely DO NOT stand without the boot, so I did not take it off at night in case I forgot.
