Hello. Asking for opinions here as I'm all over the place on what to do here-- I'm 65 and in good health. Have Hashimoto's but TSH is controlled. Was on HRT for many years, but had to d/c abruptly due to 2nd spontaneous bleeding problems. Since d/cing HRT, I've had one joint/tendonitis issue after another. Had open Carpal Tunnel repair done 09/25 (which I should have done 20 years ago). Still slightly tender from scar tissue/nerve regeneration in one spot. Here's today's question. It's been over a year since I developed chronic pain in my 3rd toe. The joint at the end of the toe looks inflamed with a noticeable bump which is painful and the toe has rigidity. I've had bunions (worse on RT foot) since my 30's and was continually told by Ortho's to 'leave them alone' even when I had periods of chronic pain--no one ever mentioned the long-term risk of arthritic joints, etc). At this time, I have no pain in my bunion, big toe or 2nd toe (forgot to mention that I have Morton's Toe, the root of all evil as it turns out), but every so often I'll go through a period of metatarsal pad pain that will last for months and then disappear. None currently. I've seen 3 Orthos/1 Pod re this and have settled on the Ortho, I believe. He's reading the MRI results this week. During my 1st consult, he said he's prefer to do MIS if correcting the bunion, but dependent on the arthritic findings, fusion of the big toe is the best correction. I am so freaked out about having this toe fused, I can't tell you. As a side note, unless I'm reading the results incorrectly, it seems strange that the Radiologist doesn't refer to the 3rd toe? The tech put a 'bean' on my toe and in Reason for Study it states 'Pain in Third Toe on Right Foot'. Based on the MRI results, what if I just have the 3rd toe fused to correct the problems with joint/pain relief and don't correct the bunion/toe which isn't painful? On the other hand, I also don't want to be 75 and trying to deal with the larger surgery. As a side note, I just retired and really dread the idea of a long post op period of limited mobility and discomfort. I also see that my left foot has issues of its own (thanks genetics) which probably should be addressed at some time. Ok, I'm getting more worried writing this all down. BTW, I worked in medicine for 38 years and I'm not sure why this particular surgery is bothering me so much! Thanks for any advice. Moderate asymmetric narrowing of the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint is seen with osteophyte formation at the margin.
