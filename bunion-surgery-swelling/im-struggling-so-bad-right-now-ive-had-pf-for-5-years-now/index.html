FYI, So far, this has helped me. I currently have minimal issues. Note: I have wide, flat feet with bunions ugh! *What works for one person may not work for another. Rest is very important. The following is in alphabetical order to a certain degree. ANALGESICS Advil or ibuprofen COMPRESSION SOCKS / STOCKINGS I was wearing 20-30 mmHg thigh high Compression stockings before I came down with Plantar Fasciitis. I continue to wear them as they help with circulation. I also wear Injinji toe socks. They help prevent your toes from rubbing and being squished up in a standard sock. DIET I try to keep my weight down. Although, that is a constant battle. I limit alcohol, sugar, and non-organic foods. If you crave dark chocolate, but want to limit your sugar intake, check out cacao. I purchase / drink Crio Bru. They have a variety of flavors from around the world. Don't waste your money if you don't like dark chocolate or are going to add sugar. It tastes best if made in a French Press. Website: https://criobru.com/ EXERCISE I have cut back on my walking. I was up to walking my dog 4 miles a day. I think this is what precipitated the Plantar Fasciitis. I was walking 2 - 2-5 miles a day. I am slowly increasing to 3 miles a day. Also, I try to do some yoga, pilates, weight work, and ride an exercise bike. I break it up and do it a little at a time throughout the day so I don't overstress my feet. FOOTWEAR Hiking Boots I have found Orthofeet hiking boots comfortable. I purchase the wide style. Sandals Revs They also have slippers. Start out by wearing them for 15 minutes and then gradually increase time so you don't overstimulate your foot/feet. They may initially hurt a little. But once you are able to increase the time, it feels like a massage. I wear them around the house all the time. I wear my Asics Gel Nimbus 25 sneakers outside when walking the dog. Sneakers Asics Gel Nimbus 25. I purchase the wide style. Note I usually wear 2 - 4 different footwear throughout the day. I start and end the day with Revs. I wear Asics or hiking boots when walking the dog, hiking or going to the store. Work boots in the garden. GELS / CREAMS Arnica, Aspercream, Biofreeze, Procure Epsom Salt Rub, Traumeel, or Voltaren Gel. I try not to use the same product for more than two weeks as I believe it's effectiveness diminishes. HOMEOPATHIC PAIN PILLS Formula 303 tablets (homeopathic muscle relaxant found on Amazon.) HOMEOPATHIC RUB Sore Muscle Rub. I believe the compression helps. Purchased on Amazon. INSERTS / INSOLES I found the inserts recommended by the podiatrist to be very painful as did my sister. I haven't returned to using them yet. I believe my foot was way too inflamed for them to be helpful. I may try them again, but for only a few minutes at a time and gradually increase the time. PAIN PATCHES When not using the gels or creams I place several Salonpas patches on my foot at night (I wear socks when doing this so they don't come off while I am sleeping.) My physical trainer has told me the biggest issue he sees is clients not holding the stretch long enough to actually help. So, hold each stretch for longer than you think is needed. Don't do too much at one time.

Community comment#63

Make sure to move your feet before standing. Ankle swirls, toe crunches and wiggles, toe points, etc. Anything that can warm up the fascia before you bear weight. The fascia contracts while we sit or sleep. The movements before standing help warm it up and give it more flexibility. Sometimes it takes a good while of diligently doing things before you will see results. My PF did NOT get better right away. There was nothing I did that gave instant relief. I was diagnosed with severe PF and edema in the bone. I also have spurs. I have written this to others and just cut and paste so I do not have to type it again. I'm attaching below. There are other things too, but this is the start on a good path and also may be the only things needed. Warm up the fascia before standing each time. Especially before getting out of bed and taking those first few steps. Ankle swirls each way a few times, toe points and holds, wriggle toes and learn to wriggle big toe independently, toe spreads and crunches, move feet back at forth like windshield wipers and like you are conducting an orchestra, also bring toes up on their own and hold a few seconds release and repeat. Do these gently at first and increase as tolerated each time BEFORE standing. You can do these laying down or seated. May not feel comfortable at first as you are using parts of the foot that are weak. It's just moving your feet before bearing weight and warming up and preparing the fascia to stand and move. The other thing to try is taping (many videos on YT on this with different techniques) and/or compression socks that support the arch. I use Dr. Motion socks but there are plenty of those types you can choose from. At the end of the day, try foot soaks in Epsom salts. Soak in very warm water(this brings more circulation to the feet where cold can restrict) and move feet around in the water, especially spreading toes and holding as tolerated. Massage each foot as well. If you try the above diligently for 30 days without any improvements, you may need to add to it and move up the legs, work on calves and knees, and into the hips with hip openers. Yoga works the entire body fascia (which is all connected) and has helped me very much. Start with chair yoga first, which is non weight bearing and will help condition you first before moving to a mat. When wanting to try toe separators, before buying anything to see if they may be for you, just use the foam separators you use to paint nails. Those are pretty inexpensive or you may even have them on hand already. Want to try the socks before buying? Put cotton between toes and cut holes for toes in an old pair of socks to hold cotton in place. This will also allow to customize the spread so it's not too much, and works much better for those who have webbing, and you can skip toes that have too much webbing in-between. With any toe spreader, you need to wean yourself into using them or they can cause more pain than good. A few minutes a day at first and you increase your time a little every few days. The best thing to do is to learn to spread your toes on your own and strengthen your foot and not depend on these indefinitely if you can help it, but these can help people learn to do so.
