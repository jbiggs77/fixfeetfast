Comment spotlight by member, Kathi Kitz: “Make sure to move your feet before standing.

Archive date 2026-10-147 archived replies
Post-Surgery Swelling & InflammationPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot ExercisesFlat Feet & Arch Support

Comment spotlight by member, Kathi Kitz: “Make sure to move your feet before standing. Ankle swirls, toe crunches and wiggles, toe points, etc. Anything that can warm up the fascia before you bear weight. The fascia contracts while we sit or sleep. The movements before standing help warm it up and give it more flexibility. Sometimes it takes a good while of diligently doing things before you will see results. My PF did NOT get better right away. There was nothing I did that gave instant relief. I was diagnosed with severe PF and edema in the bone. I also have spurs. I have written this to others and just cut and paste so I do not have to type it again. I'm attaching below. There are other things too, but this is the start on a good path and also may be the only things needed. Warm up the fascia before standing each time. Especially before getting out of bed and taking those first few steps. Ankle swirls each way a few times, toe points and holds, wriggle toes and learn to wriggle big toe independently, toe spreads and crunches, move feet back at forth like windshield wipers and like you are conducting an orchestra, also bring toes up on their own and hold a few seconds release and repeat. Do these gently at first and increase as tolerated each time BEFORE standing. You can do these laying down or seated. May not feel comfortable at first as you are using parts of the foot that are weak. It's just moving your feet before bearing weight and warming up and preparing the fascia to stand and move. The other thing to try is taping (many videos on YT on this with different techniques) and/or compression socks that support the arch. I use Dr. Motion socks but there are plenty of those types you can choose from. At the end of the day, try foot soaks in Epsom salts. Soak in very warm water(this brings more circulation to the feet where cold can restrict) and move feet around in the water, especially spreading toes and holding as tolerated. Massage each foot as well. If you try the above diligently for 30 days without any improvements, you may need to add to it and move up the legs, work on calves and knees, and into the hips with hip openers. Yoga works the entire body fascia (which is all connected) and has helped me very much. Start with chair yoga first, which is non weight bearing and will help condition you first before moving to a mat. When wanting to try toe separators, before buying anything to see if they may be for you, just use the foam separators you use to paint nails. Those are pretty inexpensive or you may even have them on hand already. Want to try the toe socks before buying? Put cotton between toes and cut holes for toes in an old pair of socks to hold cotton in place. This will also allow to customize the spread so it's not too much, and works much better for those who have webbing, and you can skip toes that have too much webbing in-between. With any toe spreader, you need to wean yourself into using them or they can cause more pain than good. A few minutes a day at first and you increase your time a little every few days. The best thing to do is to learn to spread your toes on your own and strengthen your foot and not depend on these indefinitely if you can help it, but these can help people learn to do so.”
