Scarf and akin osteotomy recovery: what 275 discussions say

Scarf and akin osteotomy recovery time, walking, the boot, screws, swelling and shoes, built from 275 archived discussions where people shared their own recovery.

Personal experiences, counted and quoted. Not medical advice.

Scarf osteotomy and akin osteotomy are bunion operations that come up again and again when people compare notes on recovery, and they are very often done together as a scarf and akin osteotomy. This guide brings together 275 archived discussions and the 973 replies inside them where people described their own scarf and akin osteotomy recovery: how long it took, when they could walk, what the boot and the screws were like, and what caught them off guard.

Because many posts mention an osteotomy in passing, the same discussions also include people recovering from chevron osteotomy, Lapidus, Lapiplasty and minimally invasive (MIS) bunion surgery, often with an akin added. Those stories appear here only as comparison points. Everything below is what people reported, with counts taken from the archive; it is not medical guidance.

What is a scarf and akin osteotomy?

In the archived discussions, people usually write scarf and akin as one procedure, shortened to scarf/akin, scarf akin or akin scarf. As posters describe it, the scarf part is the bunion correction itself, and the akin is a cut in the big toe that several people said was done to straighten the toe. A few called it a wedge cut, and in a thread about a toe still curving at 10 weeks, a reply wondered whether a more aggressive angle on the akin cut would help.

Akin is the most-named procedure in this data, with 144 mentions, ahead of Lapidus (94), Scarf (87) and Chevron (48). That is largely because the akin rides along with many bunion corrections: people described chevron and akin, MIS bunionectomy with akin and Lapidus or Lapiplasty with akin, as well as scarf and akin. Many also had hammertoe correction, Weil osteotomies on the smaller toes or tailor's bunion correction in the same operation, and that extra work shaped their recovery.

How long does scarf and akin osteotomy recovery take?

There is no single number in the discussions, and people were quick to point out that recovery times differ from procedure to procedure. The recovery updates in this data run from 9 days to 15 months after surgery. They cluster around 6 and 7 weeks, when people were asking about leaving the surgical shoe or boot, and around 6 months, when longer look-back updates appear.

Several people said swelling and stiffness hung around well after the early weeks; among the most-replied threads is one about stiffness and swelling at 8 weeks. A person who had a chevron osteotomy on each foot, months apart, said the first foot was almost 100% healed 10 months later but could still swell after a lot of time on it.

Some compared their scarf osteotomy recovery time with minimally invasive surgery. Someone 8 days after a bilateral scarf and akin asked about MIS patients back running at 2 weeks, and a reply elsewhere said they had read studies suggesting regular and MIS osteotomies have almost the same success rate, with the regular kind taking a little longer to heal.

When can you walk after a scarf osteotomy?

Walking is the third most common theme, raised in 136 of the 275 discussions, and the answers varied more than almost anything else. A person who had a bilateral scarf and akin described weight bearing in post-op surgical sandals from the day of surgery, and a person who had a scarf with a Reese osteotomy posted that they were heel-walking straight away. Others described getting around on crutches (23 discussions), a scooter (21) or in a cast (10).

The boot comes up in 53 discussions. Questions included whether anyone else had been released into a wedge boot with no weight restriction. A person with a scarf and Reese procedure admitted walking a little without the post-op boot after the sutures came out, not realizing they should not have, so it is worth asking your surgeon exactly what your boot rules are.

Uneven walking caused its own trouble. Around week 3 after an akin scarf osteotomy with hammertoe correction, a poster described strange pains across the foot and lower leg from walking unevenly, and a later thread asked when people stopped limping.

Pain, swelling and numbness after scarf and akin surgery

Pain is the most common theme in the data, raised in 168 of the 275 discussions. Posts covered nerve blocks, nerve pain on day one post-op, and pain elsewhere in the foot as people changed how they walked. Tylenol (10 mentions) and ibuprofen (7) were the pain relievers named most, and gabapentin had 5 mentions.

Swelling comes up in 88 discussions, and people paired it with elevation (50), ice (49) and compression (26). Swelling still featured in updates months after surgery, and a person who had a bilateral scarf with akin wrote on surgery day that they were keen to start icing.

Numbness comes up in 29 discussions, stiffness in 22, sleep in 16 and infection in 14. The hardest stories sat at the far end of the timeline: a person 15 months after a Lapidus and big toe osteotomy described the osteotomy area failing, permanent nerve damage and loss of almost all big toe movement.

I had a chevron osteotomy and I had my foot wrapped for 4 weeks and was advised to elevate for that entire time.

Are screws removed after a scarf and akin osteotomy?

Hardware is the second most common theme, raised in 148 of the 275 discussions. Scarf and akin patients described screws in the bunion correction plus a screw or pin in the big toe itself, a scarf and Reese patient listed 2 titanium screws, and a person with a chevron and akin said they had a screw and a clip.

Removal came up in a few different ways. A revision surgery that included an akin osteotomy also took out hardware from an earlier MTP fusion, a person 7 weeks after an MIS bunionectomy with lesser-toe work posted a week after pin removal, and a thread asked whether anyone had had their bunionectomy screw taken out. Second surgery or revision comes up in 31 discussions overall.

Whether screws are meant to stay or come out is a good question to ask your surgeon before surgery, because the archived answers depend heavily on the procedure and on what else was done at the same time.

When can you drive, work and wear regular shoes after a scarf osteotomy?

Shoes are the fourth most common theme (94 discussions). Moving from the surgical shoe to regular shoes was a milestone people asked about around 6 weeks: a person with an MIS bunionectomy and akin osteotomy expected to be cleared for regular shoes at that point and asked whether physical therapy (53 discussions) had helped anyone.

Hoka was the shoe brand named most (14 mentions), including a post celebrating parting ways with the post-op shoe for a Hoka trainer, followed by Brooks (9) and Altra (7).

Driving comes up in 24 discussions, and which foot was operated on mattered: people who had the right foot done asked how long others waited to drive. Work comes up in 61 discussions and getting back to exercise in 70; some of the most-replied threads were about an active person's recovery expectations and a 3-mile walk at 6 weeks after MIS surgery.

What people wish they had known before scarf and akin surgery

Not every story ended well, and the discussions are candid about it. Among the most-replied threads is one asking whether people regretted bunion surgery. In a thread about a big toe still curving at 10 weeks, several scarf and akin patients answered, including one who said the bunion came back very quickly. Another said their scarf and akin went wrong and left the big toe floating and pushing against the second toe, and in a separate post a surgeon proposed a Lapidus and hammertoe surgery after an original scarf and akin.

Extra procedures done at the same time were a regret that came up more than once. A person 21 weeks after an akin and scarf said they regretted also having a Weil 2,3 osteotomy, and another reply said the Weil 2,3 osteotomy done alongside an akin and scarf had overshortened the second and third toes.

The other side is just as present. A person whose first scarf osteotomy went well was having the other foot done with the same surgeon, and someone else said they could not wait for their second scarf osteotomy. Across both kinds of story, what else is done alongside the scarf and akin is the detail worth going through line by line with your surgeon.

I had scarf/ akin and wore a spacer for ages but the toe was never straight and I have quite a curve still

Questions people ask

How long does scarf and akin osteotomy recovery take?

The archived discussions don't give a single figure. Recovery updates in the data run from 9 days to 15 months after surgery, with the most activity around 6 to 7 weeks, when people were moving out of the surgical shoe or boot, and around 6 months. Several people said swelling and stiffness lingered for months after the early weeks.

When can you walk after a scarf and akin osteotomy?

In the discussions it depended on what each surgeon allowed. A bilateral scarf and akin patient described weight bearing in post-op surgical sandals from the day of surgery and a scarf and Reese patient was heel-walking straight away, while others described crutches, a scooter or a cast.

Do screws get removed after a scarf and akin osteotomy?

Hardware comes up in 148 of the 275 discussions. Scarf and akin patients described screws plus a screw or pin in the big toe, and a scarf and Reese patient called their screws permanent hardware. Removal came up alongside revision surgery and pin removal from other toes, so whether yours are meant to stay is one to ask your surgeon.

When can you drive after a right foot scarf osteotomy?

Driving comes up in 24 of the 275 discussions, and people who had the right (driving) foot done asked how long others had waited. The threads hold personal experiences rather than a set timeline, so this is one to confirm with your surgeon.

Is akin osteotomy recovery different from scarf osteotomy recovery?

The discussions rarely separate them, because the akin is usually described alongside another correction. Akin is the most-named procedure in the data (144 mentions), paired with scarf, chevron, Lapidus, Lapiplasty or MIS surgery, and people's timelines reflected the bigger procedure and any extra toe work done at the same time.

How does chevron osteotomy recovery compare with scarf and akin?

Chevron has 48 mentions in the data and is often paired with an akin as well. Chevron patients reported a mix, from excellent results that took longer than hoped to a recovery described at 8 months as really up and down. A reply in a scarf and akin thread pointed out that recovery times differ between procedures.

Can a bunion come back after scarf and akin surgery?

Some people reported that it did. In a thread about a toe still curving at 10 weeks, a scarf and akin patient said the bunion came back very quickly and another still had a curve after wearing a spacer, and second surgery or revision comes up in 31 of the 275 discussions. Others described good results and went on to have the other foot done.
