Community comment#1

You have something called RETRONYCHIA. I’m absolutely sure as I had it. It is not fungus. Antibiotics are not a treatment for retronychia. You need a topical steroid, a steroid injection or nail removal. It’s highly misdiagnosed. In retronychia, the nail stops growing but a new nail begins to grow underneath the old one, pushing the old nail up and back into the proximal nail fold. This causes inflammation, redness, pain. Retronychia is essentially when the entire nail is ingrown at the back. Signs include a nail that seems to stop growing, loss of cuticle, proximal fold redness/swelling/pain, pus, distal nail lifting, xanthonychia (yellowing of the nail). It takes a LONG time to recover, like months-year because toenails grow slowly. Nail removal is last resort. Nail removal can lead to disappearing nail bed and distal nail embedding.

Community comment#3

The nail looks thick and slightly deformed which is likely causing the skin infection. They might be able to partially surgically remove it at the sides if you wish to preserve some of the nail, to stop it from being ingrowing. It's possible if you don't have any nail surgery that the surrounding area will continue to be repeatedly infected. Mild toenail fungus can be treated with curanail but once a nail is thickened and deformed it will not grow normally again.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#12

it was very difficult to get help. I had one dermatologist that prescribed clobetasol propionate 0.05% ointment which took the swelling and pain down. I later had a Kenalog steroid injection into the proximal fold but I probably didn’t need it. My nail started to get pushed out about 2 months after I started the steroid topical. I had 2 layers of nail (the new nail pushed out the old nail). It took me 6 months to get the proper diagnosis from a nail dermatologist. My nail is now back to normal but it took >1 year from start to finish. *keep in mind steroids should never be used long term and cause skin thinning/atrophy and other changes. I may still have very slight fat atrophy on top of my toe but it’s hard to tell. I used the steroid cream too much and the injection was overkill. I should’ve just been patient.
