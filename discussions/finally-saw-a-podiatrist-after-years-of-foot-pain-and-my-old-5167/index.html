Finally saw a podiatrist after years of foot pain and my old primary not taking me seriously.

Archive date 2026-10-0925 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryMinimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Lapiplasty 3D Bunion SurgeryBest Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot Surgery

Finally saw a podiatrist after years of foot pain and my old primary not taking me seriously. New podiatrist suggested minimally invasive Arthrex Bunionectomy, starting with the right foot as that one is worse than the left. I forgot to ask her (and I will ask prior to the surgery that’s scheduled for Feb 26th) but my bunions are likely the result of my ankles being pronated. If that’s the case, I would guess it’s a much higher chance that my bunions return and if so is it worth the surgery? I feel like I’d probably need surgery again. But as things are now I walk every step in pain so not doing it will just keep me in misery. Here’s my right foot, didn’t get a pic of the left foot X-ray. And my poor little second toe has fallen victim to my giant big toe crowding the limited space

I would absolutely never get the traditional bunionectomy. Look at the difference in these two pictures. The first one, my foot my surgery,Lapipasty Solve the root of the problem by rotating over the metatarsal bone that had rotated upwards which is what causes a bunion. No bone is shaped off under my toe. Instead the bone is rotated back over fused to keep it there, And the big toe joint is rotated back in place so that you are not working on your sesamoid bones Bones which are those two knobs sticking up. The other style is cutting a bone in half and using just two screws to hold it in. Remember all the weight of your body is on that part of your foot. Your forefront of foot takes an awful lot. And also if it’s in your area I would really recommend to look into something called Syndesmosis I was instead of a fusion it uses stitches to hold the bone over in place and then you’re put in proper orthotic so it doesn’t roll over again along with other rehab rehabilitation. I wouldn’t trust your primary care she didn’t listen to you to begin with. That is so disheartening. Good luck. Just remember to investigate as much as you can.

Have you tried everything else first? For example, do you wear Altra running shoes or Topo Athletic shoes or Lem’s or any footshaped footwear? Have you had sessions with a physiotherapist? If the situation is severe then it’s wonderful that there are surgical options, but it’s not an easy option and there are risks. Community comment#5

Minimally invasive bunionectomy is a great option. Lapidus or Lapiplasty may help to correct your flatfoot to some degree and correct the bunion at the same time. If you have a moderate to severe flatfoot, consider having it all done simultaneously as you are correct, the flatfoot condition contributes to bunion formation. Odds are that no matter what, your bunion would never recur as bad as it is now with any procedure you choose and you will more than likely alleviate your pain. Just talk about these options with your surgeon before surgery.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#10

Well, Lapiplasty is in fact a traditional type of bunionectomy. It’s just done at the base of the bone rather than the head of the bone. There are a number of factors that your surgeon will look at in deciding which region to address. I’d also add that the Lapiplasty is really just a Lapidus but uses a jig. As well, it’s not true that you must be non weight bearing for 8 weeks. I was part of a multi center study looking at early weight bearing with Lapidus and in fact patients did well with WB 1-2 weeks post op in a walking boot.
