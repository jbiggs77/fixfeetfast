I think I can kinda relate to what Ariel was going through, because since my MIS bunion surgery, about 8 weeks…

Archive date 2026-09-1714 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryMinimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Post-Surgery Swelling & InflammationBest Shoes After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After Surgery

I think I can kinda relate to what Ariel was going through, because since my MIS bunion surgery, about 8 weeks ago, I have yearned to be a part of the human world again so I could walk, run, and play all day in the sun. I can't quite run, but I can comfortably walk and play in the sun if it isn't over 24°C (75°F). The zingers are like mosquito bites; I have an occasional random visit, but they are far less frequent than before, and the swelling at the base of my toe is getting better. I don't have full range of motion through the big toe, but walking feels normal and my Zombie shuffle is no more. I can't quite do a forward or reverse lunge, but I can pretty much manage my other exercises, which include using my dumbbells, core/mobility, and carrying all the groceries from the car with no help, assistance, or sympathy from my family! The biggest issue is trying to act like both Prince Charming and Cinderella, because it's taken me a while to match the right shoe to a foot that only I could love. I used to be a regular-sized UK 11 (US: 12 / EU: 46), but through trial, error, and a great Amazon return policy, I've found that I'm now a UK 11.5 (US: 12.5 / EU: 47) and 4E width. This has given me enough room for my longer second toes, and enough space for my toes to spread. Although, I have to now wonder if my bunions are genetic, developed through ill-fitting shoes, or a combination of the two? My in-person follow-up appointment with my surgeon is in early October; however, after another follow-up call, I feel like there ain't no mountain high enough, valley low enough or river wide enough to keep me from continuing to make progress. Take good care of yourselves, my friends.
