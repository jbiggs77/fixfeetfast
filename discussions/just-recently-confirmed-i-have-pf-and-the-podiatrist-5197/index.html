Community comment#8

I recommend avoiding high- impact activities until this gets better (so no running). I would also not walk without shoes on any hard surface since that will further irritate the issue. Oofos were a game changer for me, so I wear them wherever I can. For any sports activity, I recommend Brooks Ghost which have good support and a wider toebox. New Balance are a close second, and they come in wider widths (I use the extra wide size; they surprisingly don’t look wide at all but give you plenty of extra toe space). I would still replace any regular closed-shoe or sports-shoe insole with one specific for plantar fasciitis (those from Dr. Scholl are effective but inexpensive; you can get them anywhere, like pharmacies, Target, Walmart). Replace them about every six months if you use them a lot. Good luck! 😊

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#19

Whenever you get new shoes, especially orthotic ones, such as Vionic, Oofos, or Birkenstocks, be sure to break them in properly. Wear them for 1 hour on day 1, 2 hours on day 2, and so on. They are reshaping your feet, which takes time and needs to be done slowly. I’ve had 5 or 6 pairs of Vionics and I break each pair in slowly. Set a timer so you don’t forget to change out of them. Do this until they are comfortable.

Community comment#34

At home, I wear crocs with foam inserts. They slip on and off so easily. I wear them out to casual things, too, like shopping, taking my dog out, etc. I can’t put my feet on hard floors at all without support. New Balance were good for me. I need arch support. It’s good to get fit for walking or running shoes at a place where they measure your foot and watch your gait as you walk.and make recommendations . If there is nothing like that near you, any shoe store should be able help recommend. I went through a lot of trial and error trying to find the shoes that gave the right support. It’s so individual with brands and styles and feet all being so different. Once you try shoes on, you’ll know when you’ve found the right fit. It’s like a big ahhhh. There are videos on YouTube for taping your foot with sports tape. It seems strange but does give some people lots of support and pain relief. Just never putting my feet on hard floors without shoes brought me the most relief. Also foot and leg massage videos on YouTube for plantar fasciitis . There is so much trial and error at first. What works for one person might not work for another. These are the inserts I got for my crocs I also found these hip flexor stretches helpful. Good luck
