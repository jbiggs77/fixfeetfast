MIS bunion surgery recovery: gentle movement approach

Archive date 2026-09-2621 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryMinimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Scarf & Akin Osteotomy

I’m 50 and based in London, UK, and I finally have my MIS bunion surgery scheduled for the end of this month. After consulting with two different surgeons, we agreed that MIS was the most suitable option for me. My bunion is moderate rather than severe, my toe still has good mobility, and my soft tissue is healthy — all indicators that the deformity can be corrected safely through tiny incisions without the larger exposure required in open surgery. Staying physically active is important to me, and my surgeon believes that gentle movement will play a key role in my recovery. I also feel confident knowing that my surgeon is highly experienced with this technique. Social media can sometimes feel like a place where "polarised opinions" dominate, and I’m certainly not promoting this method over any other. Every case is individual. But if you’re considering bunion surgery, speak directly with medical professionals, ask questions, and gather as much information as you can so you can make the decision that’s right for you. I do plan to share my journey here, because I genuinely believe it will be a positive one, but after living with my bunion for 2 decades, I'm glad to finally be taking action.

Community comment#8

I had MIS 6 months ago and it went well. I pretty much stayed with foot elevated for two weeks, only up to bathroom week one, and 5 minutes a day week 2. I took ice to surgery and iced and elevated on the way home, and iced every 20 minutes per hour the first week, then 4 -5 times a day. Get lots of ice packs so one is always ready, and I had a box with a foot hole cut out to keep the bed covers off my foot. With keeping the swelling down by not standing, I had no pain, and after the prescribed drugs were finished, needing no pain meds. Good luck with the surgery. It has made a huge difference to my walking without pain
