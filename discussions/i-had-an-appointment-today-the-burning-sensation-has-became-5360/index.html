Community comment#1

I had the arthrax (I think that was what it was called) minimally invasive surgery. They used propofol to put me to sleep and it was done as an outpatient and took about an hour. My foot was wrapped in an elastic bandage and I went home with a wooden sandal. I had no pain. The following Monday (surgery on Friday) I returned to have bandage changed and was given a boot that went up about one foot. Still no pain. It was always wrapped in an elastic bandage. I never had any pain but it got somewhat infected and i ended up having to take two very strong antibiotics which did a job on my stomach for two weeks. If u want to see photos, I have it documented on Pinterest. I can send u a link.

Community comment#2

Yes, surgery is painful and expect a 2-3 month recovery. Mine has gone longer than that. Yes, they put you asleep. I could heel walk afterwards in a cast shoe. I was in regular shoes by one month post-op. But nowhere near healed by then.

Community comment#3

I'm 75. I had minimally invasive by podiatrist that was on the Doctors show. no pain. One month in surgical boot, but could walk on heal after surgery, then pin removed, after 1-2 weeks after pin removal I wore Skechers. Swelling starts going down after 8 weeks, but still a little swelling. I'm wearing a sandal now. Everyone's different.

Community comment#4

I had both feet done 9 months apart. For me both were successful. Boot crutches for 6 weeks, no driving. After 3 week check I could put weight on cast around the house. I fully understand that burning sensation especially when wether is damp. 6 months recovery slow but worth it.
