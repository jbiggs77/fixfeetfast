Warning: Long Post Alert! I've honestly tried to edit but there is a lot to say... I had MIS bunion surgery, in the UK, on my right foot about 3 months ago, so I thought I'd give an update, especially for those who might be considering this technique. But please remember, although I'm not as tough as Rocky Balboa, I've always had the eye of the tiger and enjoyed the thrill of the fight. I suspected I had bunions in an era when you had to go to Blockbuster Video to rent movies on shiny discs called DVDs. For the most part, my bunions caused me no problems; I went through life just fine, even becoming a runner in the process. I tended to dismiss the mild mannered warning signs, but eventually my bunions embraced the dark side and would bring me to tears and a few choice words. So, you can understand my bias when I say: I don't recommend waiting until you experience "pain" before taking action. Bunions are a structural issue, and you wouldn't buy a house if only part of the foundation were sinking into the sand or drive a car with a tyre that's almost flat. But... "Weight bearing" is NOT the same as walking with full range of motion. You won't be Livin' da Vida Loca in two weeks, but you can move like a zombie extra from the Walking Dead. In my case, it took about a month to walk normally. At 3 months post op I can tip toe with full range of motion just fine (dorsiflex?) but I don't quite have full planter flexion in my big toe, but I'll continue my daily exercises and foot strengthening...but my right foot is functional again. So now the lecture.... If you're gonna to do this, you MUST PRIORITISE your recovery. If you're unsure, take MORE time than LESS. PLEASE DO NOT go back to work too soon, unless you work in the beds & furniture department of IKEA and you have to demonstrate comfort and elevation for most of your shift. Take GOOD care of yourselves my friends. P.S. Apologies if you've read this already I had to post again due to technical issues.
