Community comment#1

Podiatry vs Ortho in USA only 1. Podiatric surgeon trained in last 15 years will usually have a 3 year residency in only foot and ankle nothing else ! ( Yes there are some brief rotations in other areas ) train with orthos and podiatrists 2. Ortho - they do a one year fellowship with only a few orthopedic surgeon who teaches them the same thing they learned with little variety. They do this after 5 years of learning general surgery and general orthopaedic surgery. So they may or may not have seen a lot of foot and ankle work and only trauma not elective surgery like bunions. 3. Podiatry procedures performed typically in residency is over 1500, Ortho is maybe 300 at most as it’s only a year and little variety of seeing other docs. 4. Podiatry can be board certified in foot and ankle surgery but it must be by American Board of Foot and Ankle Surgery Orthopedist’s CANNOT be board certified in Foot and Ankle surgery only in general orthopedics ( this is a big difference) 5. Ortho docs do not have a major focus on biomechanics, Podiatrist’s do! All of this is a generalization and there are always going to be exceptions. I have seen nightmare results from both sides as well as excellent results from both! It is about specific training, passion for work and experience every time...You just have to do the homework. I was taught by both and have taught both.

Community comment#27

There is no such thing as a podiatrist/orthopedic surgeon. Many podiatrists like to market themselves as orthopedic surgeons but they are not. They went to podiatry school. Not medical school. Orthopedic surgeons are medical doctors who did a a 1 year residency in general surgery and at least 4-5 years in orthopedic surgery followed by a year of foot and ankle specialized training! Very competitive and difficult to get into the field. Podiatrists went to a podiatry school (not a MD).
