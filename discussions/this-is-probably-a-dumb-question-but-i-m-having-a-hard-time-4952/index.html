This is probably a dumb question, but I’m having a hard time wrapping my mind around keeping my toes above my…

Archive date 2026-09-2917 archived replies
More foot health experiences

This is probably a dumb question, but I’m having a hard time wrapping my mind around keeping my toes above my nose for 2 1/2 weeks. Thank you to everyone that posted the ideas on the foot elevation pillow. I can see where that is a great idea. I’m thinking the only way to accomplish toes above nose is by laying flat -otherwise there’s no way I’ll have my toes above my nose. ￼ I can’t imagine having to lay flat for 2 1/2 weeks 24/7 though. I naïvely thought that I could sit on a sofa with my feet propped up on an ottoman with a wedge pillow or something. ￼I could see laying down flat with my head on a pillow, trying to read or watch TV starting to ￼bother my neck over time. I’m curious how others applied this in their own lives during the daytime hours?￼ as you can probably guess reality & panic is starting to settle in…TIA!

Community comment#1

I stayed in or on my bed for the first 2 weeks and kept my foot elevated on a wedge pillow. It wasn’t always elevated above heart level during the day, but was at night or when I was sleeping. I would move between a sitting position and a more lying down position during the day.

Community comment#2

I didn't have my foot above my nose (2.5 weeks post op). Just kept it up as much as possible. Very little swelling.

Community comment#3

Large fluffy pillows. But it's just elevated above the heart level to keep the swelling down. There will be comfortable positions, just have blankets and pillows wherever you are. I bought a Sleep Number bed which made my recovery a lot more comfortable with a Zero Gravity setting.

Community comment#4

Elevated is the key. Mine was rarely "toes above the nose."

Community comment#14

It’s not a dumb question. Not at all. I kept my feet elevated above heart level about 75% of the time for the first 3days post-op as the “toes above nose” position was really uncomfortable for me and actually caused more throbbing pain. Especially in my toes! For those who are bothered by laying in that position, doing it for about 20-30 minutes every 2-4 hours and prn (as needed) is good enough to aid in improving blood circulation, decreasing the development of blood clots and reducing swelling while laying flat. I just didn’t find it necessary for me to be completely uncomfortable all day long. Breaking it up throughout the day worked best for me. *I’m no doctor. Just stating what worked for me!*
