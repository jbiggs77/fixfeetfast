Hi everyone, im currently on the waiting list for a scarf osteotomy with akin osteotomy.

Archive date 2026-10-2318 archived replies
Post-Surgery Swelling & InflammationPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After SurgeryScarf & Akin OsteotomySurgery Complications & Wound Healing

Hi everyone, im currently on the waiting list for a scarf osteotomy with akin osteotomy. The surgeon has already said this will involve hardware of screws. I have 100% made my mind up that the surgery is right for me, I can no longer continue with the pain. Surgeon has also recommended im a good candidate. I've noticed post op guidance seems to differ depending on country or region. Im UK based, Scotland. I've been told once home as a day case, im on strict bed rest for 3 weeks, no walking unless to toilet. Surgeon says this is the best to avoid swelling and minimise pain. After that start toe exercises, use heel and crutches for movement after that period with the usual check ups at 2 and 6 weeks. I've read many others have been instructed to move as soon as you can. 3 weeks in bed is obviously very restrictive, my head is full of how to wash, wash hair etc? How was the pain afterwards? I live rural, so do rely on driving. How long did you have to wait to drive, I've been told expect 6-8 weeks if recovery goes smoothly. Thanks for reading.

Community comment#1

I’m now 1yr post-op. I was sent home w/a protective barrier over my incision, followed by a simple wound dressing and in a surgical wedged shoe. I was heel walking on day 1 for toileting purposes, getting food/water and getting up to move around in order to prevent blood clots. Due to a nerve block, I initially didn’t have any pain. The block lasted just shy of 3 days. When I did start experiencing pain, it was noticeable but tolerable. Approx days 4-11 were when the pain was bothering me quite a bit. Breakthrough pain is where💩 got real for me. Whew! Regardless of taking the prescribed narcotics and OTC meds on an alternating schedule, it still found a way to show up and that’s when the 😭 started flowing. Applying cold therapy behind my knee and directly on the surgical site did help with alleviating the pain. So, be sure to take your pain meds religiously! I was able to drive 3wks post-op. I had surgery on my l-foot, so that was a great feeling! Will you be sent home in a cast, boot, splint or shoe? Wishing you a successful procedure and a positive recovery journey.

Community comment#2

I’m in the uk, same op. Discharged with crutches and protective shoe. Rested for 2 weeks, did get out of bed and sat in chair downstairs with minimal movement. After 2 weeks bandages and stitches removed and toe splint used. Moved around a lot more. After 6 weeks straight back into my pre op trainers. 12 weeks now and completely back to normal, gym, swimming, 10,000 step and no swelling. Fingers crossed for you, I know I’ve been very lucky.

Community comment#3

I had this exact surgery. I think for first 2 weeks I was told to do minimal walking, and I was in much pain and ended up using crutches, so couldn't have done much if I wanted other than go to bathroom, etc. . After week 2.5/3, I was allowed and did walk in a stiff orthopedic shoe, and after week 4/5 I was instructed I must walk in my sneaker since the healing was going well, as it would create other compensation issues with my feet, which is absolutely true, I began having soreness in my other metatarsals. I think using crutches after 3 weeks is a little much unless you ahve that much pain.

Community comment#5

Hi, I’m based in London and 3 weeks ago I had the Lapidus Arthrodesis procedure. Day surgery (local anaesthetic 😮). I was non weight bearing for 2 weeks with aircast boot (when up with crutches) and allowed only bathroom visits in that time with foot elevated either in bed (night) or in living room (day). In theory could have showered with seat and limbo waterproof cover but told easier and safer to strip wash. It’s not that bad. For hair I am paying a local hair dresser to wash and dry it for me once a week (thankfully my hair doesn’t get too greasy). I know it’s an indulgence but it has been money truly well spent! After 2 week x-ray and check up, healing going well so now in trainer (needed to use a shoe horn at first to help get in it) and can put light weight on foot (25%) slowly increasing this over coming weeks. Wearing a tubular bandage to help with swelling. I can now have short baths but told not to get the wound wet for too long. No showers as not allowed to stand but if you have a shower stool that would work. The first few days the pain was bad, I’m not going to lie, but not 100% of the time and I tried to listen to music with headphones to help distract me. Overall the first 2 weeks are the worst then it really does get easier. Yes I have also been told 6-8 weeks for driving. I think it’s going to be nearer to 8 for me. Can’t see me driving in another 3 weeks but you never know! Good luck and all the best for your surgery and recovery.
