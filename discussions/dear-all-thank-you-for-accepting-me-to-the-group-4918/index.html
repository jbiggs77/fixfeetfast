Community comment#3

Yep, it *does*, not *can*, affect the rest of your lower body. Basically, your feet are your anchor. I have cavus foot a.k.a. high arch feet. Consequently, my calves are tight, my knees are screwed up and my hip flexors feel like steel rebars. 🙁 Your feet control the weight distribution throughout all the other lower body muscles and joints. Physio exercises are pretty much the only way to keep things loosened. I have a bunch of exercises that I know I should be doing every day. But the thought of having to do physio every day for the rest of my life is depressing. I've had high arches since I was a kid. If they're not fixed by the time you're 8, you're doomed to have problems later in life. My high arches caught up with me in 2020 when I was 56. Now every step I take is painful and I have chronic ankle instability. I suggest seeing a podiatrist or chiropodist about your problem.
