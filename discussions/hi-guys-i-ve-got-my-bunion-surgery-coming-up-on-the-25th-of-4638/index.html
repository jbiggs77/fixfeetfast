Community comment#1

I had mine done July 21st. I’m doing fantastic. Make sure you stay on top of your pain meds for the first 3 days. On the 4th day zero pain for the rest of the healing process. The hardest was having that huge cast on for 2 weeks . Use a knee scooter to get around. I’m 69 and living alone I had no issues what so ever. I went into a surgical shoe instead of a boot . So happy it’s over it was worth it. I was even able to get a pair of regular shoes on today. I didn’t walk in them though . I’m still using the surgical shoe. Most people used ice packs a lot I didn’t . I did keep it elevated. I started driving after I got the cast off with no issues. You’re not supposed to but I did. I have no pain and walk normal with no limp. I had a great surgeon. Best wishes and you’re going to do great!!!

Community comment#8

I will be 2 years post op on the 18th, my advice take one day at a time, be patience with yourself, and be kind .The first three days were the hardest but take your pain meds even though you don’t feel anything as once the nerve block wears off you might have pain.. I had major work on my foot so it was painful, but once you get pass the first week it will be better.. Elevate 24/7 with an elevation pillow, get up move around every 1/2 go for a few minutes. I did sponge baths for the first week as my bath tub/ shower set up was difficult but felt good the following week.. I washed my hair under the sink as I had a great walker with a stool and good brakes .My house was full of stairs but once I made it up there to our bedroom I stayed for a week as bathroom was right there .. I had a great hubby who brought all my meals to me .. Just allow yourself to feel all the emotions.. it’s hard doing everything on one leg .. good luck you will do great ..

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#21

I did research for a while. I looked at lots of posts here to get ideas from others. Some things I personally recommend: Knee scooter and crutches kept in strategic places (my scooter couldn’t go into our toilet room for example, so I kept a crutch in there. ) Elevated leg pillow (the 2 leg one) Shower chair. (If you have a movable shower wand get a new sticky holder for it and place it lower than the original one) Lots of ice packs. Surgery pants- or tear away pants. Waterproof sock sleeve thing for when you are able to shower. Also the day before your surgery remove ALL obstacles, rugs, etc. just think through how you’re going to safely navigate your home.
