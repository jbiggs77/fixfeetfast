Community comment#25

Depending on how rugged the terrain will be on the mountain, you should probably invest in a trail shoe or even a hiking shoe/boot. You definitely don’t need the super squishy marshmallow feel under your feet because that won’t provide the stability and protection you’ll need along the way, so I’d stay away from a road shoe. If you enjoy the feel of your OOfos with the meta rocker feel and want to combine that feel with a trail shoe then check out the New Balance More. It’s still soft under your foot, but sturdy to withstand the elements and to protect your feet! Has a more weather protectant upper as well. It also accentuates the heel/toe motion through your foot like OOfos. If you need to kick it up a notch and get a hiking shoe, then I’d suggest some Hokas. Their hiking line up will be cushioned, but have more aggressive outsoles and a variety of heights around your foot/ankle: low, mid, high top. I have a pair of the Hoka Anacapa Mid GTX and they’re amazing! They’re gortex with a Vibram outsole. They literally feel like a road shoe that’s actually a hiking boot! They’re lightweight, but with longevity and cushioning! Maybe go to a REI or other outdoor store to explore some options because I don’t want to limit what you could try to only two styles. Just wanted to tell you some options that feel similar to OOfos on the foot. Also know that every style of OOfos is built with the same exact outsole. If you find a shoe or “boot” in their lineup, then it’ll be built on the same footbed as the slides/flips flops and probably wont accommodate your “hiking” needs. I just recently lost my mom as well. Grieving with you. 🫶🏼

Community comment#28

Oofos have a lot of cushion. You’ll need to gradually increase the demands on your feet so you streangthen then AND allow them to heal. I hope the hike is not too far or demanding. If you can find a sneaker with a wide toe box, medium cushion for stability with comfort, then put in inserts as appropriate it may work. A shoe like Hokas with high cushion may be most comfortable but unstable and not enough grip for your hike. I’ve been using new balance walking shoes because of relatively low foot drop, wide toe box, they come in wides, and have medium cushion. That shoe is a possibility, or maybe one of the others is best. Add an insert — a dr shoals or similar may work fine. In the beginning with so much heel pain I needed max cushion in the heel. Now I’m not having pain, so I removed the orthotic with lots of heel cushion and I’m using one with a lot of arch support but no heel cushion. (that’s where I’m noticing pain after a few hours at work. I hope you can buy a shoe and wear it for say a hour or so well BEFORE your hike. That way you can exchange it for something else if you need to AND you can work your way up to a shoe with less cushion and less heel drop than other shoes. Either way pls do exercises to strengthen and wean yoiurself off of the Oofos slowly. They feel great, but won’t solve the underlying issue. I recommend Dr Angela also
