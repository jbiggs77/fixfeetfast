A sprain is damage to the ligament, usually a tear (the degree is usually graded). Once that heals, it isn't ever the same again, is weaker, and will tear more easily. This gets worse the more times you do it, which can ultimately result in a broken ankle since the ligament prevents you from going that far (it offers you some protection).

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#8

Strengthening muscles can help offer support to the ligaments, but it's not a fail safe. Unfortunately, with weak tendons, you also end up with tense muscles because they are doing the job the ligaments are supposed to do. This is seen pretty commonly in people (like me) who have disorders such as Ehlers Danlos Syndrome (I have the hypermobile version). Stronger muscles in that area can act like a brace...but...ultimately an actual brace during certain activities (long walking, jumping, side to side motion like in hiking) would be recommended. Sometimes you might be able to get away with compression socks that have calf compression, if the instability is mild.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#13

Did they give you contrast with your MRI? If not...it may not show. Your ligaments ARE like rubber bands...or are supposed to be. But the more damage, the less stretchy they become (scar tissue isn't stretchy). And no, it doesn't permanently stretch them like that...that would take repeat and prolonged stretching. Google "Sprain Grades" to see the differences between them.
