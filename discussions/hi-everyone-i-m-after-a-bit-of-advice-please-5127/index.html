Archive date 2026-10-0816 archived replies

Hi everyone, I'm after a bit of advice please? I have a partially torn achilles tendon which the docs won't do anything about unless it ruptures. It's getting more and more painful and I can't take pain meds other than paracetamol. I've been looking online and it says the best thing to try and build up strength is calf raises on the stairs but I also have osteoarthritis in my big toe joints which makes that exercise almost impossible for me. Does anyone have any other strengthening exercises that I can try to strengthen the achilles and try and reduce the pain? Also if anyone has any ideas on how to relieve calf tightness, that might help reduce the pain too. My calves are super tight all the time due to my unnatural walking gait. I try to massage them myself, soak in the bath, stretch them out, etc but they're always tight. Many thanks for any ideas to try!

Community comment#3

Like others said, rest and heal first. For rehab when possible: Try using soft and super soft foam rollers from OPTP (occupational and physical therapy products; from their website or Amazon). Not only can you roll up and down your legs you can do a side to side movement called “shearing”. That really helps my tight calves which can actually cause one of my knees to lock up. There are melt method videos on YouTube about this. That can really loosen up your legs and tight muscles and fascia. Once I loosen my calves, no more knee pain. It can be done gently by you - but you’ll need to take care of that Achilles tendon (bandage it?). You can also try the medmassager or the Hyako R1pro massager- these are gentle vibration tools- not percussion massagers. Also, consider using a stretch strap to stretch your calves (when you’re able). OPTP also has a pro model stretch strap and instructions. A higher priced version would be the castleflex stretch strap - but it would be easier to overdo it with that, considering your injury. Try those. Good luck

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#13

the issue with tendons is that they don't respond well to immobilisation, which a boot does. In fact, this can make them worse unless they are ruptured. Have you had an MRI or ultrasound to confirm? See the work of Jill Cook who is a tendon expert and who explains progressive loading is the way to recovery and there is pain involved in rehab. Many links online, eg https://theprehabguys.com/rehab-achilles-tendinopathy/ If you are young, you should be able to recover well pending on the exact nature of the injury, as I understand it.
