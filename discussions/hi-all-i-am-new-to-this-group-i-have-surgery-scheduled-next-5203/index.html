Hi all, I am new to this group, I have surgery scheduled next week for an interposition arthroplasty on my…

Archive date 2026-10-127 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryMinimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Best Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After Surgery

Hi all, I am new to this group, I have surgery scheduled next week for an interposition arthroplasty on my large toe which is bone to bone which I chose this procedure instead of having a fusion. My own tendon tissue will be placed between the bones. I also am having a large bone spur, shaved and mild bunionectomy . I cannot wear any shoes comfortably for any period of time other than a flip-flop or a very soft wide tennis shoe . This has been going on for a long time and has gotten worse, with a lot of discomfort. I was anxious to get this done, now I am scared. I am reading that foot surgery is so very painful and I don’t do well with pain, but I feel I need to get this done. I’m interested in hearing from anybody who has had the interposition arthroplasty. My surgeon has done over 1000 of this surgery. He is very competent. He does not use a boot for recovery. He instead straps the toe and foot for 30 days. I need the same surgery on my right foot as well other than no bone spur and the bunion is larger . Any input regarding pain control and recovery would be much appreciated.

Community comment#1

I am in day 16 of bilateral bunionectomy and chevron osteotomy and while I had a lot of concerns doing both at once - given what I read about pain, truthfully, I am really glad I did both at the same time. The first 3-4 days were the toughest but not unbearable as I kept up with my pain meds. I think every situation is different and of course, I am no expert on these procedures. Maybe I’m just fortunate not to have much pain, not sure. While there is worry and concern, what is the alternative? For me, it was progression of the bunion and limited mobility as I got older. Surgery was a risk I was willing to take, hoping my future mobility will be active. Good luck to you and I hope you have a successful surgery and easy recovery 😀

Community comment#3

Get prepped. Get a knee scooter, a wedge to prop foot/leg up, ice packs, pre prep meals if possible, get protein powders to use while in recovery, if you have a shower buy or borrow a bench to sit on to bathe. Read all you can on all the support group has to offer on past experience it’s an awesome place to see 1st hand Info. Blessings to you on your ❤️‍🩹 recovery

Community comment#4

I would be absolutely sure of your Drs protocol for pain meds before the surgery. With the opioid crisis today, you certainly want to know how your post op pain will be handled.

Community comment#6

It’s good to hear your surgeon is experienced and competent with your procedure. Be sure to follow his advice carefully with recovery. I think it is a good choice to do one foot at a time. Hopefully it will be easier to manage any pain, discomfort and mobility. I had surgery 3 weeks ago on one foot. Different surgery to you (minimally invasive chevron akin bunion surgery, correction of 2nd toe alignment and bunionette). I only had mild pain for the first few days and took Panadol. I used crutches to keep off my foot completely for the first 2 weeks, even though I was told I could weight bear. I think this helped to eliminate any pain. I have used a wedge pillow to elevate the foot, a shower chair, and a bed cradle to lift the blankets off my lower legs. Good luck with your surgery and recovery.
