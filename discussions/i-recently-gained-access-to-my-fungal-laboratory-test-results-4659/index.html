I recently gained access to my fungal laboratory test results from a year ago.

Archive date 2026-09-1618 archived replies
Surgery Complications & Wound HealingToenail Fungus Treatment

I recently gained access to my fungal laboratory test results from a year ago. I have had onchomycosis for 10 years or more and to say I am more frustrated than ever. Unbeknownst to me, what grew on my culture was a non-dermatophyte called Alternaria species. It is a mold (not a fungus) and responsible for less than 15% of infections. Alternaria lives in the environment. Commonly found in soil, and also known as black mold that can be found in your house. It starts with an injury to the toenail, allowing the organism to enter the broken nail and skin. I had such an injury 10 years ago. Over time it spreads to all the other toenails. Best line of defense is Itraconazole or one of the conazole family of drugs. Unfortunately due to a serious interaction with one of my daily medications, I can't take the preferred treatment. I am currently on my third round of Terbenifine, which is supposedly effective but not as well. Yes I am checking liver function via bloodwork every few weeks. I am also using Jublia topically, which happens to be ketoconazole so thats good, however I dont have a lot of faith in topicals even though I grind my toenails right down to the bed. I have done all the home remedies, old wives tales and potions. All the vicks, listerine, apple cider, tea tree oil, red light therapy, laser, iodine in the world won't help a deep seated infection where the matrix under the nail is affected. Apart from having them removed this is my last battle. It might take years of terbinifine and Jublia (and alot of $$$ spent so far!) but Im determined to have nice feet again! Sorry for the long rant, I know you all understand how frustrating it is. Moral of the story...find out what the organism is on your fungal culture and make sure you are getting the right treatment!
