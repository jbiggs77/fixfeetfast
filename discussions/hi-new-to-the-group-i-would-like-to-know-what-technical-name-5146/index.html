The technical term for the position of your toe would be a valgus deviation of the 2nd distal phalanx.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#4

The position of your toe is not a condition per say, its more an anatomical variation or positional change. The closest condition would likely be a subluxation or dislocation of the last joint of your toe, however like i mentioned this can be an anatomical variation. The best option would be to speak with an orthopedic or podiatric surgeon as each surgeon has techniques they like and prefer, and they will be able to provide the best advice regarding function vs aesthetic correction. X-rays may be helpful to determine what's happening at the joint and would provide more details about your options for surgery. Hope that helps 🙂 , sorry I can't provide an exact diagnosis.

I had the joint removed and a permanent compression screw is buried in the fusion. It affects your gait (much more than you would expect)! My surgeon advised me to do that cause he said the toe would start curling again if he did anything else. That’s not true and he should have been sued, the jerk. . That’s all I’ve been able to wear since the first surgery 5 years ago. That’s why I think the soft tissue procedures allow you to get back into shoes more comfortably. Get several opinions.
