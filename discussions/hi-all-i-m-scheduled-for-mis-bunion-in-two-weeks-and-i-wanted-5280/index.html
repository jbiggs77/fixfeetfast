Minimally Invasive Bunion Surgery (MIS)Best Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot Surgery

Hi all! I'm scheduled for mis bunion in two weeks, and I wanted to get everyone's thoughts on nerve block vs. no block. This orthopedic surgeon, a foot and ankle specialist who does these every week, does not routinely use a nerve block, and his patients walk "right away" if they wish. But I'm concerned about pain management. Not an opioid fan, either. They have said I can have the block if I want it. Thoughts? My bunion is mild to moderate but beyond the point where I can wear closed-toe shoes without pain, even wide box.

Community comment#4

I have not had the surgery. But this is my concern as well. I am leaning toward NO NERVE BLOCK because there is a small risk of nerve damage complications if you have the block (nerve damage is also a concern from the surgery but I don't want to add to this by having the block). Also remember to ask your doc where to ice; you don't generally ice the foot (but you can - I've seen ice slippers on Amazon!); ice packs go behind the knee. Good luck!

Community comment#8

I had MIS surgery in January. No nerve blocker and very little pain. I took ice to the surgery and iced and elevated immediately on the ride home. First 48 hours only up to bathroom with elevation and ice 20 minutes of each hour. Rest if first week walk downstairs to couch ( and elevate) once a day. Week two I stood 5 minutes at a time three times a day.

Community comment#10

My surgery was September 16th. No nerve block. I wasn't really given a choice. I took a few steps walking out of the hospital and it was fine. I feel like I have a pretty high pain tolerance but it wasn't that bad. I made sure to keep my pain medicine going every 4 hours for the first 3 or 4 days. I took ibuprofen with my pain medication as well. It was tolerable but I could tell when it was about half an hour before my pain medicine wore off. It would start to throb and hurt but it still wasn't horrible. I slept a whole lot. My worst pain wasn't even where my bone was cut, mine was in the joint of my big toe. He did some extensive work to tighten up the capsule, so I guess that's expected. Good luck either way you decide to go! You got this!
