Hi. When chronic i was not weight bearing so I did full body Pilates (mat work not standing). To keep as fit as possible. I do Pilates every other day and go to a class. I’m 64. The exercises that helped me with the PF/heel spur: - wall stretch (even if you get odd pains carry on with this I did it 6-10 times a day when chronic) AM/PM… - intrinsic foot exercises as tolerated (Google this) - heel raises starting low reps and seated (build up difficulty It’s important to build up gradually. Your feet / ankles will probably be weak. So if you storm ahead things can become worse as they did with me. No I did not have an injection. They do not always work. Persons having one are at greater risk of a facial tear in the 6 months following. I just thought whatever I do I’m going to have a period of waiting to see if it works so o decided no interventions and just physio. Good luck. 🤞

Community comment#63

Oh, boy. Classic pf has a pain in the heel, why does your PT person think it's not. You can also have pain in other areas. I wish I found dr. Angela walks pf protocol before I ever went to 2 useless podiatrist and 1 orthopedic Dr, and received open plantar fasciotomy with PRP injections that I would have never got if I found her information first. Look her up, you might not be ready for a lot of her foot strengthening exercises yet this soon after your surgery. Probably starting with toe yoga and a lot of sitting foot and calf movements. It took me a long time to be able to walk after my surgery. PT helped me get back to walking, then I found a sports chiropractor who did ART- active release technique which helped with a lot of leftover pain i was having. Listen to Dr. Angela walks video on shoes. Inserts and shoes like brooks and hoka are just a crutches. We need to strengthen our feet, not put a "crutch" on them. When you do try a different shoe, for me it was Altra Paradigm, but you may need to start with 4mm drop shoe like the Altra fwd, especially if you're used to brooks or hoka high heel drop shoes. Other people seem to also like topos, I just haven't personally tried those. But, when you transition to a new shoe, you have to do it sooooo slowly. Dr. Angela talks about how to transition also. It comes down to a lot of consistent foot, calves, hip strengthening exercises, the right shoes, wearing toe spacers for shorts spurts of time barefoot at home, and scraping using a graston tool- scaping is a hard technique for me and my sports chiropractor used this and dr angela has videos on it as well. Any other pain I used voltaren cream during work, soaked my feet in warm epsom salt at night, and I used castor oil overnight, others use magnesium oil... Good luck!!

Community comment#82

My pain started on my right heel about 2 months ago when I got out of bed in the morning and walked to the bathroom and maybe a few more steps. I didn’t have hardly any pain the rest of the day. Someone told me to wear a “United Ortho Plantar Fasciitis Adjustable Leg Support Brace (bought from Amazon) boot” to bed at night. The boot is supposed to keep the foot in a “L” shape so it should not fall or drop - hope you know what I mean. It took a few nights to get accustomed to it but I’m a side sleeper so after three nights I could sleep thru the night. The pain was gone after three nights. I still get a little heel pain and a little tingling in my foot during the day if I sit for a long time and then walk. I have been happy with Skechers “Go Walk Arch Fit - Grateful”. I have tried several different brands arch support shoes. Sometimes I use a different arch support insert. I have not found an insert that I like. I’m open for suggestions. Once in a while if I sit too long my feet will swell a little. Recently I started wearing short compression socks and that has seemed to be helpful for pain in my arch area (don’t know why). I’m hopeful I can stay ahead of the pain and be able to walk outside when the weather is warmer.
