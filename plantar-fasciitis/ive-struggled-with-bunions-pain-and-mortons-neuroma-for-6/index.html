Community comment#4

I’m so sorry you really do sound like you’re at your limit with this. After finally getting your bunion and Morton’s neuroma pain under control, having this burning heel pain suddenly show up in both feet has to be incredibly discouraging. Plantar fasciitis is definitely one possibility, especially if the pain is worst with those first steps after getting out of bed or after sitting for a while. But burning pain can have other causes too, so I wouldn’t automatically assume it’s plantar fasciitis. Since it’s happening in both feet and is significantly affecting your life, I’d have a podiatrist take a look and make sure they’re considering things like nerve irritation, heel-pad problems, Achilles issues, or other causes of burning foot pain. In the meantime, I’d avoid walking barefoot on hard floors and try to stick with supportive shoes around the house. Gentle calf and plantar fascia stretching can help some people, but I’d start gradually rather than aggressively stretching an already angry foot. Ice after activity may also take the edge off if it feels good to you. I also wouldn’t give up hope just because changing shoes hasn’t helped. Getting the right diagnosis makes a huge difference in figuring out what actually needs treating. I picked up Reclaim Every Step; Your Journey to lasting Relief and Recovery from foot and Ankle Pain by Tamira L. Sholvette MD from Amazon after my Rheumatologist mentioned it, and I found it helpful for understanding the different things that can contribute to ongoing foot and ankle pain. I know you’re desperate for relief right now, but people absolutely can improve from persistent heel pain. Don’t let anyone convince you that you just have to live with it keep pushing for an evaluation and a treatment plan that actually addresses the cause.
