I began thinking that maybe my arch had dropped because I've always had high arches.

Archive date 2026-09-294 archived replies
Post-Surgery Swelling & InflammationPain Management After Foot SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot ExercisesFlat Feet & Arch SupportPlantar Fasciitis Treatment

I began thinking that maybe my arch had dropped because I've always had high arches. I did some research and found this: A dropping arch is directly linked to plantar fasciitis. As your arch flattens, it stretches the plantar fascia (the thick, rubber-band-like tissue connecting your heel to your toes). This over-stretching creates extra tension, leading to tiny tears and inflammation, which is known as plantar fasciitis. Fallen arches are structural and usually cause a dull ache along the inside of the ankle and midfoot. Plantar fasciitis is an inflammation of the tissue and causes sharp, stabbing pain, especially in the heel when you take your first few steps in the morning. Anyone else been told about arches being related to plantar fasciitis. My doctor never even mentioned it.
