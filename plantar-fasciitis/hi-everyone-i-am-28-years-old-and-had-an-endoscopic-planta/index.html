Archive date 2026-10-1928 archived replies
Pain Management After Foot SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot ExercisesPlantar Fasciitis Treatment

Hi Everyone, I am 28 years old and had an endoscopic plantar fasciotomy in September 2025. I had relief after the surgery but in February of this year began to experience pain again. I have done cortisone shots, a cortisone shot with immobilization, physical therapy, etc. I went for an MRI and prior to my surgery the fascia was measuring at 4mm thick and now is measuring at 9mm thick. Has anyone experienced this before? I have gone for a second opinion and have been given all sorts of suggestions from a custom made AFO, shockwave therapy (not covered by insurance), platelet injections (not covered by insurance), and performing a second procedure but performing it open this time. If anyone has any advice it would be greatly appreciated!

Community comment#10

Sorry you’re dealing with this. A couple things stand out the fascia being 9mm thick after surgery is pretty significant, but I’d be curious whether the MRI showed active inflammation/scarring, a partial tear, or just postoperative thickening. Thickness alone doesn’t always tell the whole story. Before doing another procedure, I’d probably want to know exactly why the first endoscopic release failed. Was enough fascia released? Is there scar tissue tethering it? Is the pain actually still coming from the plantar fascia, or could it be something like a nerve issue, plantar plate issue, tendon involvement, etc.? I’d be cautious about jumping straight to another surgery without a very clear reason. Shockwave and PRP are often discussed for chronic plantar fasciitis, but results can vary, especially after a prior release. Have you had someone who specializes in failed plantar fasciotomy/revision cases review your MRI? Also, where is your pain now compared to before surgery (same spot or different)? Also, I’m really curious what made you decide to try surgery in the first place and what exactly you tried to correct the problem before you even resorted the surgery
