Yes, it is. That's what its supposed to be doing. Making your body think your foot is hurting so it sends help to heal it. If you've had FP for sometime your body forgets about healing it, so needs a little help.

Community comment#2

I also had shockwave therapy to treat my plantar fasciitis. It took about five sessions. I started the therapy on the lowest setting, and by the third, I was on the highest setting. In terms of pain, there was gradual pain building up until I got to the most advanced setting. It’s been two weeks since my last session and my plantar fasciitis pain is almost 90% gone. He did do the shockwave therapy on my calves also. That made a big difference in terms of pain I was feeling. But it helped a lot, I think it was shockwave therapy that got rid of this plantar fasciitis for me. I tried stretching rolling a ball going for physiotherapy acupuncture. Nothing else worked. ￼

Community comment#3

It took until my last session (5 weeks) for me to feel better, but then I felt a lot better. While getting shockwave, do not take any anti-inflammatories (like ibuprofen) and baby your foot. Pause PT exercises, just gentle stretching, no long walks, hikes or running. This what really helped me

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#10

what my podiatrist said is that shockwave works, in part, by getting your body to restart the healing process and part that includes allowing some level of inflammation. Anti inflammatories shut that down. Also too much exercises causes the healing to become disorganized so the new fibers won’t to strong. His advise was to stay off the anti inflammatories, baby my foot, and use a night splint
