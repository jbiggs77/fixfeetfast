Hi, I’ve been suffering with plantar fasciitis for over 5 months now, and I’m honestly getting to the end of…

Hi, I’ve been suffering with plantar fasciitis for over 5 months now, and I’m honestly getting to the end of my tether with it! My podiatrist advised me to do some stretches, which, to be fair, I was already doing, but they haven’t really made much difference. I was also advised to stop running to give it time to heal, but strangely enough, my heel seems to have got worse since I stopped running. I’ve spent a fair bit of money on decent shoes and various things to try and help, but nothing seems to be working. I’m still struggling with the pain and it’s incredibly frustrating when all I want to do is get back to some sort of exercise. Can anyone help with how long it might take to improve and was there anything that genuinely helped? I’m desperate to get back to exercising and would really appreciate any advice or experiences you can share. Thanks!

Community comment#2

It varies. Some people have had it 3- 10 years. I had it 5 months really bad. Stopped all the "exercises" that overloaded or over stretched the fascia. As it caused flare ups. I did wear a night boot for 9 or 10 months keeping my foot neutral and preventing my foot from pulling down. I started scraping 4 or 5th month the inner calf by knee. The whole calf and arch. I started isometric holds sitting for 45 seconds in the morning before isometric loading a couple weeks later. End of the 5th month I improved following what I mentioned above. This is month 11. But I've been pain free 6 months with one small small issue a couple months ago that lasted a day. So there's quite a stretch in the time it takes to heal. If i was a runner. I would give it a shot at the 11 month mark in my recovery. As I felt it could turn sideways from month 6-10. Note: it's a load issue mainly. Best wishes.

Community comment#21

As a fellow runner I can sympathise. Mine took around 8 months to heal. The first thing that helped was wearing orthotics in all my shoes and never going barefoot. Secondly, switch to whatever exercise you can, I cycle and row (indoor) and both are good cardio substitutes. Finally you need specific strengthening for your foot and calves (plenty of example on YouTube). I did those exercises every day. The improvement was very gradual as was my return to running but you just need consistency and patience.

Community comment#24

I am on the mend. Have had it for about 5-6 months. The biggest advice from my physio was to watch my load. Meaning cumulative load, walking, exercises etc. over days not one day. I also started alternating massage physio, and once I started taking it seriously and treating it as an injury is only when I started seeing progress. Strength training, prescribed moves from physio along with stretching and rolling. It’s slowly getting better!
