Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#27

Hi Dorcas, there are genetics that influence if some of us are more prone to developing them- hypermobility is an example. So yeah, they run in families. Bunions are avoidable though even if there are genetic conditions that run in the family. Weakness and footwear that is tapered in the toe area in both women’s and men’s footwear contribute to bunions. A tapered shoe directs the big toe to centre and constricts all of the toes. Wide toe box footwear like Altra Running Shoes or Lem’s provide space for the toes to splay. We’re born with feet shaped like pizza slices, and footwear shaped like pizza slices is best 🍕🍕

Community comment#32

Bunions are usually genetic. You need to see a podiatrist and get x-rays of your foot. Surgery is different for everyone. Some people heal on the given time frame and some don't. If you have a desk job, you will be off at least two weeks. I was 7 weeks non-weight bearing. I am a retired teacher so it wasn't a big deal for me. Your bone will need to be cut. It takes a year to heal fully but by the end of three months months most people can do a lot on their feet. There are things that can go wrong after surgery. Nerve damage is a big one. It is rare but can happen. I am 13 weeks out now and so glad I had surgery. I can walk barefoot again and my podiatrist cleared me Monday to resume all activities. Surgery is not something to take lightly. I am glad you are asking questions now. Pain wasn't bad for me at all after day three. Swelling of your foot is usually pretty manageable after 12 weeks. You may need a plate and screws in your foot. I have them but I don't even notice them. See a podiatrist who can tell you exactly what you will need and what recovery time for you will be like.
