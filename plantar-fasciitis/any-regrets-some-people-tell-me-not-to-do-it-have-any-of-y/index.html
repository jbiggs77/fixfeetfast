Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#33

These are good habits for everyone to build strong feet and a robust orthopedic foundation to live an injury free life. Most shoes, including fancy well known running shoe brands like Hoka, Nike, Addidas, OnClouds, Sketchers are terrible for you. Anything you can find in a shoe store. The toe box in the shoe is rounded on most sports shoes, for example. We started wearing rounded toe boxes and raised heels 550 years ago during Europe’s first middle class. Finally normal people were able to afford horses as transportation and riding boots meant you were a person of high social status. Riding boots had a rounded toe box to help us fit into our stirrups and heels to help us not slip out of our stirrups. These “technologies” have made it into your sports and running shoes as well, surviving long past the usefulness of horses as transportation. Even some “recovery footwear” has it. The implications are people are at risk for bunion, plantar fasciitis, overlapping toes, hammer toes and more. The orthopedic damage that these running shoes do is awful. Cushion, and arch support, which gets larger and larger every year acts like lidocaine at the dentists office and encourages our arch muscles to relax which causes them to lose strength. That puts you at risk for arch collapse (AKA flat feet). Westerners and those in the industrialized world have incredibly weak feet. That’s because we put foam running shoes on kids and never let them gain the foot strength that they need in later life. We all need to undo the orthopedic damage by strengthening our feet. Improve foot shape, range of motion, dexterity, and strength through challenging our feet. That’s the point of the rock mat, toe spacers, and sensible footwear with 3 things. 1) foot shaped toe box 2) flexible sole 3) zero drop (Flats!) design Also barefoot around the house, and for extra credit go barefoot on trails and challenging surfaces.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#36

the human body has a remarkable ability to recovery. Surgery was a big step and if you have no regrets then a big positive step 🤩🤩🤩 and we have the whole rest of our lives to build STRONG feet! Feet that can kick a soccer ball barefoot and that can carry you confidently over challenging terrain. Heels in the office are no good but heels in sports shoes aren’t any better. The 5 millimeter drop and thick stiff sole can be improved. The drop shouldn’t matter eventually because humans don’t naturally have their heels touch the ground when we walk. Habitual barefootedness is common in Australia and their walking gait is different than ours. Habitually barefoot people walk on the ball of the foot as opposed to the heel. So heeled shoes can only interfere with our goal to learn to walk without our heels touching the ground. Happy to send you a barefoot walking technique guide! The rock mat is more challenging than the masseur sandals but similar idea. The best thing to do is to walk barefoot on a rocky trail to stimulate your nerves in your feet and start building protective muscle through hypertrophy! It will take years to build up to it.
