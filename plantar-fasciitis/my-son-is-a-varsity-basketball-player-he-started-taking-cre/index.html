My son is a varsity basketball player.

Archive date 2026-10-1425 archived replies
Post-Surgery Swelling & InflammationPain Management After Foot SurgerySurgery Complications & Wound HealingPlantar Fasciitis Treatment

My son is a varsity basketball player. He started taking creatine and protein supplements in March 2025. Within weeks, beginning in April 2025, he began experiencing significant pain in his shins and under his feet. Around the same time, he also developed a severe fungal infection between the last two toes on both feet. Over many months, his symptoms worsened. We consulted multiple orthopedic sports doctors, physical therapists, and chiropractors. He was diagnosed and treated for plantar fasciitis and shin splints, underwent extensive therapy, used expensive orthotics, took medications, and even had surgery on his feet for the infection. Despite repeatedly mentioning to every doctor that his symptoms began shortly after starting creatine, we were told that creatine is safe and could not be causing these issues. Finally, in February 2026, we met a doctor who immediately recognized that he had Chronic Exertional Compartment Syndrome (CECS). He underwent bilateral surgery on both legs, and during recovery, the doctor asked him to stop taking creatine. During that period, he started feeling significantly better. This week, he restarted creatine—and almost immediately, the pain under his feet returned intensely. After further consultation, another doctor advised him to stop all artificial protein powders and creatine completely, explaining that for some individuals, these supplements may contribute to inflammation, fluid retention, muscle tightness, or other reactions that can worsen symptoms. We were told that dietary changes should ideally have been explored much earlier, before so many invasive treatments. I’m not saying creatine causes problems for everyone. Many people use it without issue. But in our son’s case, the timeline has been impossible to ignore. Please, if you or your child are dealing with unexplained foot pain, shin pain, muscle tightness, or unusual symptoms—especially after starting supplements like creatine or protein powders—take a close look at diet and supplementation as part of the bigger picture. Sometimes the body is telling us something, and it’s worth listening before moving toward medications, expensive interventions, or surgery. If sharing our experience helps even one person avoid the trauma our family has been through, it will be worth it. Edit: This doctor says he treated 4 young patients who came to him with failed kidneys, damaged bones and similar problems. Root cause for all 4 is the supplemental proteins and products. Makes total sense

very good question. In March 2025, my son had started lifting weights in addition to his already intense varsity basketball training. He began taking creatine because we believed it would help improve muscle strength, recovery, and performance—like many young athletes are encouraged to do. At first, we naturally assumed his pain was caused by overtraining and strain on his legs. We had no reason to suspect the supplements. But because the timing felt too coincidental to ignore, I did my own research—including on ChatGPT—and raised the possibility with multiple doctors that creatine might be playing a role. However, every source we consulted—including ChatGPT and several medical professionals—reassured us that creatine is generally considered safe and unlikely to be the cause. So naturally, we trusted that advice and focused on treating what we were told were sports-related conditions. And honestly, teenagers don’t want to stop something they believe is helping them—especially when doctors and even trusted information sources are telling them it’s fine. Unless a doctor clearly says, “Stop this,” they usually won’t listen to a parent’s concerns. Later, I spoke with an experienced doctor from India who immediately raised concern that creatine and other protein supplements could be contributing factors. He explained that these products in some individuals may react differently, leading to complications involving muscles, kidneys, or bone health. He also shared examples of other young patients he had seen with serious issues potentially linked to these supplements. Looking back, I wish we had stopped all supplements much earlier—even temporarily—just to see if it made a difference. We trusted the widely accepted message that these products are harmless, and that delayed us from considering what may have been an important piece of the puzzle. That’s exactly why I’m sharing this now—not to claim this happens to everyone, but to encourage other parents to question everything, observe timelines carefully, and consider diet and supplements as part of the bigger picture before going through medications, expensive treatments, or surgeries.
