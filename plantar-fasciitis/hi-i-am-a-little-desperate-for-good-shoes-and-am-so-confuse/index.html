Community comment#1

If you have flat feet + PF, you usually don’t want super high arch support OR super soft shoes. You want stable cushioning + mild support. Good options to look at: * ASICS Gel-Kayano 30 (best overall stability) * Brooks Glycerin GTS 21 (softer but still supportive) * Saucony Guide 17 (lighter/mild support) * New Balance 860 v13 (good balance) Avoid super soft “marshmallow” shoes long-term — they can actually make PF worse. PF is very individual, but the goal is support + stability, not extreme arch 👍

Community comment#4

As your plantar fascia heals, some arch support is a good idea. I would switch shoes up every couple hours if you can. I always have a second pair of shoes in my car. Look for wide toe box, not wide shoes per se, just something that gives toes room to move. Topo has different styles, check their website. Altra as well. New Balance even has some shoes with a decent toe box. Avoid Nike, too narrow. You can add a basic arch support if you get shoes without. Check Superfeet or Aetrex gentle arch supports. If you need some extra stability, Brooks Addiction or Brooks Adrenaline. Switching between 2-3 of these shoes throughout the day would be helpful. Soft tissue therapy: google “active release technique near me” for a certified therapist. This helps with to adhesions and scar tissue. Graston or IASTM along with this is helpful. Maybe the therapist you find does both. Clinical sports massage therapy in calves, hamstrings, hips, peroneals, IT band to help reduce tension.

Community comment#35

Reposting my original post again… hope it can help you. I’ve struggled for almost 3 years with PF and have tried EVERYTHING (PT, taping, heel cups, insoles, walking boot, foot slings at night, chiropractic, icing, tennis ball rolling, acupuncture, cortizone shots, red light, dr anglea, new mattress… the list goes on) and nothing was working and I was desperate. I then ended up seeing an orthopedic surgeon, who did and MRI and told me both feet had tears and spurs and surgery was my best option at that point. But my body and something inside me decided that was not my best path forward. So I researched and found videos and tried a bunch more things. I now understand better what a tear means, what thick fascia means, how the fascia works and how it’s connected to everything in our bodies and that we can heal our bodies if we listen to what they need. I stopped listening to all the outside noise and went inward. And I never went back for surgeries. I’m happy to report that in the last several months I’ve found more relief than I ever have with a combination of these things below. Calf massaging sleeves, 2 times a day for 20 minutes. Neuroball, a few times a day for 10-15 minutes working on pressing into the ball and letting your fascia meld and create space in your foot for better circulation. I work my toes upward pressing down, the ball of my feet, my arches and my heels. There are exercises online. I also use this ball by sitting on the floor and working it up and down my calves to create space and decrease tightness in the fascia. I also do the some of the stretches in these videos linked below as well as clam shell (with band) for hip strengthening. The hips are a huge part of this issue for a lot of people. It’s not a foot problem, it higher up the kinetic chain issue that manifests in the feet. This ball is awesome. I’ve probably tried close to two dozen different shoes (zero drop to various stack heights and everything in between) and the only shoe that has helped is Topos. I was in Altras when my issue started and for months I could only wear padded slip ons but they wee preventing my feet from moving correctly and strengthens the intrinsic foot muscles. So I transitioned to Topos for a while and then as I got pain under control, back into Altras. (Brooks, Birks, Hokas, Altra that everyone recommends could not help me, nor Dr Angela’s protocol either.) For a lot of people who have severe (acute) pain in the beginning trying to go into barefoot wear or zero drop is not the initial answer. You must work up to it. I use castor oil and dragon balm alternating on my feet every night with socks. One last thing since last posting, I’ve added this ‘foot corrector’ tool to my routine - pricy but worth it. I have gone from literally crawling on my knees to the washroom in the mornings with pain often at 9-10 to now my pain level being between 2-3 most days, as I continue to stretch calves, strengthen intrinsic foot muscles, regain mobility in my hip flexors and strengthen my lower body. I hope something here can help you. Good luck!
