New to the group, but not new to plantar fasciitis I am in my mid 30s and have had it for over 7 years despite…

Archive date 2026-10-2389 archived replies
Best Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot ExercisesFlat Feet & Arch Support

New to the group, but not new to plantar fasciitis I am in my mid 30s and have had it for over 7 years despite constant physical therapy, orthodic insoles, arch support slippers, ice, heat, rolling my feet with a massage ball, stretching, night boot, expensive shoes, two different surgeries (gastroc recession and plantar fascia release), dry needling, acupuncture, cupping, massages, etc. and the struggle continues. I have an abnormal presentation of PF, my feet do not hurt at all in the morning and the more I stand the more pain I am in, and if I overdo it, I will pay for it for days. I can’t be on my feet for more than an hour at a time without my feet hurting pretty bad. I am also hyper mobile. I am never ever barefoot. I have arch support slippers that I do think help. I have to pick and choose what I can do/what I have the spoons for and have had to give up a lot of activities I used to love doing and felt were part of my identity (hiking, long walks, yoga, generally being active). I would love any words of support from long term PF sufferers who are also in a situation where some things have helped but nothing has ever resolved the issues and allowed me to do more he things I want to be able to do. I tend to oscillate between feeling down/hopeless and accepting that this is my life now and that I have to find ways to make it work for me. Thank you in advance! I’m also open to suggestions if there are things that have worked on persistent plantar fasciitis that I have not tried.

hi Cati, here’s a summary and an example… Professor Keith Barr of University of California is the leading edge of this research. And all top sports teams and athletes rehab their sports stars like this (ie where the money is). We do 30 second isometric holds around the connective tissue (NOT stretches) The 30 second hold is very important to overcome Stress Shielding (it is why standard eg calf raises don’t fix the injury itself they just make the surrounding area stronger) So the beginner one I show (pinned on my profile) is sitting on the floor with feet against the wall and push the wall away with the toes for 30 seconds for 4 reps with a couple of minutes rest. Twice per day, 6 hours apart. Doing this consistently got me pain free within 7 weeks of starting it (13 weeks after injury). Any questions let me know, good luck 🤞 Here is a video of one of the basic exercises from my profile...
