Long and possible crazy sounding post warning… So just curious of others thoughts.

Archive date 2026-09-2422 archived replies
Bunion Surgery RecoveryBest Shoes After Foot SurgeryPain Management After Foot SurgeryWalking & Weight Bearing After SurgeryPhysical Therapy & Foot Exercises

Long and possible crazy sounding post warning… So just curious of others thoughts. 3 years ago my foot pain started in both feet. 1st podiatrist right away said plantar fasciitis and did the usual conservative treatments. It got worse, i even heard a pop in each foot one day while going down some stairs rather quickly. The doctor did 2 rounds of cortisone shots to no avail. X-rays and an MRI according to him didn’t show any tears. Tried night splints, inserts, different shoes, PT, etc. Gave up on that podiatrist and went to another one that also did surgery. Seemed great at first. Wanted a new MRI and said my right foot had a tear in the fascia. Even though i told him my left foot felt much worse. Ended up doing a PF release surgery in 11/2020. He swore in time that i could wear regular shoes and be normal like other people. all would be good. When I complained of how much my left foot hurt, He said my left It didn’t have a tear and didn’t need surgery. Over the next few follow ups, I told him How my feet started cracking a lot in the mornings and also, and most disturbing, i started developing a new issue, A lot of calf pain and calf spasms (would feel like my muscles were dancing all Under my skin). He brushed it off as nothing more then needing time. Fast Forward to fall 2021; I can’t take it anymore. Calf pain, sciatica pain on the left side and sometimes feeling like my left leg is shorter when i walk. So i Go to an orthopedic foot and ankle surgeon. He takes a quick look at me standing and walking and says that I have the following: Posterior tibial tendonitis and tendon contracture from Overpronattion Collapsing arches And flat feet And Possible PF as well. He immediately said that i have to where stability shoes forever. No flat shoes or low support shoes ever again. And the expensive Brooks that i had just bought were not going to work. He showed me some NB tennis shoes , very high on the inside, arch area. So, new shoes once again (have lost count of How many pairs I’ve tried) and another round of PT but this time more focused on PTTD, not PF. New stretches and exercises for home Too, of course. This PT placed was much better and it did Seem to help some. Mostly the manual massaging of my aching feet and calves are what helped. But just like with everything else, it helps for the moment but as soon as i load my feet (Just participating in normal life activities and events), they hurt again terrible! And my calves as well. Feel like the calf muscle is pulled tight a lot, like a rubber band. I do a lot of self massage and it does help. I find that if i load my feet in the tennis shoes , my feet really hurt bad! I have lite ride crocs which are squishy and man do they feel so much better than any tennis shoes! I always look forward to coming home and putting them on . Went back for follow up on 2/22/22. He is sending me for another MRI, on my left foot only to check the post tib tendons but he said flat out if the shoes, support, PT and exercises aren’t working at this point, then i most likely need the major foot surgery which consists of breaking the heal bone, transferring tendons and screws, etc. it’s an awful surgery, long recovery with no guarantee of complete success / pain relief. This sounds horrible!! Especially to a full time working mom of 3 (2 days a week working from Home), and caretaker to my disabled husband . Like i can’t even see if happening until i retire in 13-15 years. Now, on the what some may believe is the crazy part of this post… my pain and issues have gotten way worse since first trying to diagnose and treat my feet. It seems like Any treatment like i have received either does nothing or makes me worse even! So I’m seriously considering trying my best to go back to any pre-pain routines. Meaning whatever shoes i wanted and all. Is it possible that in trying so hard to figure this out, i have been doing damage myself? With always messing with my feet and trying so many things? Or is my worsening pain and issues simply a sign of the time going by and the Natural progression of My feet problems being legit and getting worse with age and activity, etc.?! I hope I’m not alone in these thoughts? Can any of you relate? Or even better, provide some insight. Thank you in advance!

Community comment#7

In many ways, I can relate. I have a structural issue where my ankle joint (specifically the talus and calcaneus bones) did not separate in the womb. This fusion of the ankle joint is called tarsal coalition, and it affects everything and is causing other issues like chronic plantar fasciitis and fat pad atrophy. My ankle joint can’t be fixed, so things like tight calves, tight hamstrings, etc that are likely causing plantar fasciitis must be dealt with using a workaround. I am currently searching for a good chiropractor. It seems that fixing back issues and calve muscle and hamstring muscle issues often resolves PF from what I read in a PF Facebook group I am in. I would venture to guess that you have altered your gait in response to your foot pain and a lot of your other problems developed because of that. It may also be that back problems or calf tightness caused foot problems. The steroid injections did nothing for me for PF, and over time they destroy tissue. Chiropractors and physical therapists vary a lot, and many don’t understand the feet very well. I think asking a lot of questions about how much foot pain they have treated is in order. I think overall chiropractors understand how the whole body is tied together better than PTs. As far as shoes, I bought Hoka Bondhi sneakers and Hoka recovery flip flops. I love the flip flops. They are so soft and comfy. The sneakers are okay and better than other sneakers but not great. The flip flops cost about 1/3 what the sneakers did. I have not seen much success with foot surgery. Getting over the damage from surgery takes so long, if at all, and then you finally figure out if the surgery is successful. What is the name of the surgery recommended to you? There is likely a FB page for people who have had or are considering it. I am in a group called Everything Triple Arthrodesis, which is the surgery for my tarsal coalition condition when you can’t stand the pain any more. It would not fix my other foot issues and in general is not that successful. It has a 12-18 month recovery period. I am also in a plantar fasciitis FB group, which is very helpful. It is for other foot pain in the heel area, too. I have seen people post about the conditions you mentioned lots of times.
