Archive date 2026-09-2943 archived replies

Hello, I'm new to this group. I am 34, just saw a podiatrist because someone noticed my bunions, and recommended I take care of them before they get worse. (I have very flat feet as well). I don't have pain now, but I recently noticed I have started to get big blisters and calluses on a lot of my toes (after things like going for an hour run or wearing certain shoes). The podiatrist who looked at my feet recommended I get the surgery as soon as possible and get it over with while I'm young. My questions are- Has anyone done the "puncture" bunion surgery (MIS)? Is it even worth considering surgery if I don't have pain? Will it definitely continue to get worse? Thanks!

Community comment#4

If you decide to get surgery see a few different podiatrists and foot and ankle orthos for opinions. Ask them what procedure they’d recommend and why. You want to make sure they do elective bunion surgeries very frequently at least weekly or biweekly. And do not go with a surgeon who’s just telling you what you want to hear. I wouldn’t choose a doctor who tells me they can do both feet at once or tells me recovery will be less than 12 weeks.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#21

6-8 weeks is the time it takes for bones to fully heal, but you won’t be feeling back to running until around 3 months. I had MIS in Oct and was back to running mid-January. It takes a while for your body overall to catch up after a surgery like this- I was amazed by how exhausted I got when I started to get into more activity - even just walking or running errands. Honestly if you don’t have pain, and it’s not affecting your other toes, I would hesitate on surgery. They won’t necessarily get worse, or incredibly worse, with time. If you wear the right shoes (wide toe box, not restricting) and good insoles/orthotics, you should be able to prevent or at least slow progression as the years go on! That said, I’m happy I had the surgery (mine were painful), but the downtime and recovery is a lot.

Community reply · In reply to an earlier comment#39

How to perform foot doming: Find a comfortable position: You can do this exercise seated or standing. Flat foot contact: Ensure your heel and toes are firmly in contact with the ground. Raise the arch: Imagine shortening the foot and drawing the ball of the foot towards the heel, creating a doming effect. Maintain the dome: Hold the arched position for a few seconds, focusing on engaging the foot muscles. Repeat: You can do 10-15 repetitions on each foot, gradually increasing the hold time as you get stronger.
